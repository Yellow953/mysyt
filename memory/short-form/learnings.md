## A. Joe's standing preferences (ACTIVE)

### How we make ideas and stories
- **Story first (Joe, 2026-11-01):** compelling, accurate stories over fact-packed explainers; a few well-chosen facts; proportional research; packaging (title, first frame, thumbnail) designed at the idea stage. Evidence: the strike script (repeated food gag, weak ending) and Pitcairn v1-v2 (genetics lecture, statistic ending) were accurate but not compelling.
- **Test every proven competitor format (Joe, 2026-11-12):** after the Ricks Clips / POV Simple Finance / Paper Billions analysis Joe said "I want to try all the things that worked from the competitors." Each proven format gets its own 3-Short batch (one change per batch so results compare), in the order of the test plan in strategy.md (T1 story parable, T2 wronged-you time-jump POV, T3 news-jack). Facts stay real (hard rule): parables use REAL stories with dramatized scenes, never invented events presented as history; second-person POV scenarios are fiction like thought experiments, but every number in them is real.
- **Retention craft (Joe, 2026-11-12, "apply the learning"):** every script follows `viral-playbook.md` §13: first sentence ≤ 8 words, a through-line object that escalates and is resolved, beats linked by because/therefore/but, reorder and turn tests, a kicker that echoes the hook, open loops (Shorts: frame-0 question + re-hook at ~20-25 s; long-form: re-hook every 45-60 s and every chapter ends on a hand-over hook), line hygiene, visuals that show what the line names, and a premise contract for what-ifs. Source: Higgsfield's faceless-video manuals (research/short-form/2026-11-12-higgsfield-skills-review.md); craft rules, not yet our data. Owners: script-writer (E, G), lf-story-architect, lf-scriptwriter, lf-critic. NOT adopted (conflicts with Joe's 11-07 long-form rules): named human vignettes and "historians disagree" lines; hedge inside the narration instead.
- **Ideas come from proven competitor videos + a fresh angle**, with three stops for Joe (ideas, outline, script) and no credits before the script is approved (Joe, 2026-10-31).

- **Global history, story first (Joe, 2026-10-31):** pick historical stories by storytelling potential (something compelling happens to someone: stakes, conflict or surprising decision, escalation, reveal), curiosity, emotion, visuals and audience relevance, not geography. Mix ancient/medieval/modern and all continents (wars, disasters, escapes, money and power, strange laws and jobs, betrayal, survival, injustice). Flag repeated regions/eras among the last 15 historical ideas (`region_era` in video-log.csv), no quotas. Avoid repeating the same famous figures; lesser-known stories only when the narrative is stronger. Middle East is fine when it is the strongest story, never the default. Evidence: our first history batch leaned on Mamluk/Ottoman/Silk Road topics because the old test angle was "Middle East & Silk Road".

### Brand, topics, originality
- High energy: stakes, power, danger, money or a ticking clock. No toilet/bodily-fluid grossness as the main hook (disgust only as seasoning). Test every idea: "would Joe watch this himself?"
- Brand: Mr. Yellow Scarf, lazy sarcastic everyman, second person ("you").
- No "worst deal in history" framing or premises (Joe 2026-11-12, rejected the $800-Apple-stake idea): no sold-too-early, passed-on-a-fortune or "history's worst deal" stories, in Shorts or long-form. Replaces the old "worst deal in history (flip it sometimes)" brand line.
- Originality: never condense another channel's video or copy its script, signature devices (e.g. clapperboard red X) or art. Same QUESTION and the generic myth → proof → reframe → modern question formula are fine, with our own sources, jokes and structure. Describe styles in words; never feed another channel's art to the generator.
- Joe (2026-10-29): keep SHORTS only and the same ink visuals; test topics 3 Shorts at a time per format. Pre-approved producing the batch without re-asking (~25 credits) once he OKs hooks.
- **Thought experiments (Joe, 2026-10-29):** hypotheticals do not exist, so the outcome is imagined, and that is why they are fun. Narration commits to the imagined consequences (vivid, escalating, funny); keep the real facts real (numbers, sources) and put "this is a thought experiment / physics says X" caveats in the DESCRIPTION, not as a fun-killing line in the narration. Chosen set: Sun disappears, pencil from scratch (forest and a mine), internet dies: what can you still do.
- **Company/brand videos (Joe, 2026-10-29):** show the REAL logos (Netflix, Kodak, Nintendo, Blockbuster...) in the images. This overrides the old "no real logos" rule. Draw scenes in the company's own era and place (e.g. a 2000 US strip-mall rental store, a 1975 Rochester lab, 1960s Kyoto), not generic old/rustic settings. The image model garbles logos, so leave a blank sign/box area in the prompt and composite the real logo file locally with Pillow. Logos live in `assets/logos/` (netflix, kodak, nintendo, blockbuster PNGs from Wikimedia Commons, Joe OK'd 2026-10-29); scenes.json `logo` field draws a white-plate badge at the top of the shot (assemble.py), `thumbnail.py --logo` puts it on the cover. New logos need Joe's OK to download.
- **Fact-fix habit (2026-10-31):** Joe's own scripts can contain small factual slips (Royal Navy has no "sergeant"; a carrier crew is ~1,000-1,600, not "thousands"). Keep his text, change only the slip, and say so in script.md's fact notes.

### Cut-out animation Shorts (Joe's feedback on the volcano pilot, 2026-11-05)
- **Positioning:** keep the character fully inside the frame with margins (feet and scarf tail not at the edge), keep him clear of set pieces (not touching/overlapping the volcano), props sit on the ground line, effects (sparks, smoke, labels) must not stack on faces or each other. Check every beat on a contact sheet before rendering.
- **Grounding (Joe, 2026-11-05, 2nd round):** set pieces must SIT in the ground, not float: draw the ground layer IN FRONT of the volcano/factory/bin bases (sink them 25-70 px), add grass tufts and a contact shadow; put the ground low in the frame (ground line ~73% of the height, captions below it) so the bottom half is not empty, and make the character bigger (head radius ~115 px). Effects: do NOT draw lava/smoke/fire as plain code circles (Joe: 'sucks'); generate small effect sprites (lava splash, fireball, dark smoke puffs, 0.5 cr each on magenta) and animate them (scale, rotate, drift), with code only for small bubbles/labels. Sky-only top area: put clouds/thermometer/labels there.
- **Smoothness:** render 60 fps; every pose swap has anticipation squash then stretch; no hard cuts between locations, use slide (whip) transitions timed in a silent gap of the voice; camera moves eased.
- **Ending:** a video must end with a proper closing beat (a final line + a visual payoff), not stop on the last fact; the last frame should slide back into frame 0 to loop. Volcano pilot v2 ends with Mr Yellow Scarf recycling the bag ("So yeah. Just recycle.").

### Illustrated + animated mix, hooks, SFX (Joe's feedback on the Emu War long-form/Short, 2026-11-06/07)
- **Mix** colorful generated illustrations (Ken Burns / pans) with Remotion cut-out animation; do not make a video all animation. **Vivid color** always (saturated palettes, CSS saturate ~1.3), no faded pastels.
- **Hook:** first 5 s = the premise in one line over the most striking illustrated image, then a punch image on the key word (zoom punch + thud). Speed the hook clip ~1.15x if Grady's delivery makes the key word land after ~4 s.
- **SFX:** synthesized machine-gun / action sounds on gun moments (mg_burst, mg_short in remotion/public/emu), strongest in the hook. **Spacing:** groups of animals in fixed rows, never random clumps; keep the mascot clear of them.
- **Vertical illustrated shots:** pan across a 16:9 illustration (focus x moves) at full height; check the mascot is not clipped at the first frame.
- **Funnel Shorts:** a Short that points at a long-form video is posted 1-2 days AFTER the long-form is live and links it as the related video.

### Cut-out batch rules (Joe, 2026-11-08, from the Sun/hurricane/seawater/dig batch)
- **Every cut-out Short mixes real animation with at least ONE whole illustrated image** (style v4), used for the hook and/or the big reveal; a strong hook (premise in one line over the most striking image at frame 0, then a punch on the key word) and a DESIGNED thumbnail (big emotion, text adding to the title), not the loop frame. Whole image doubles as the cover.
- Joe edits premises toward concrete visuals ("a rocket FILLED with trash", not "throw a bag") and wants real-world context added when the answer is local (GCC desalination); length can be raised to ~60 s when the story earns it.
- **Render with `--concurrency=1 --gl=angle` (never 3):** concurrency 3 tiled/banded ~10-17% of frames with big sprites (found independently by the Sun, seawater, dig and hurricane agents); frame-scan every video and look at the contact sheet, a 'tile' look is a real glitch, not a sheet artifact. Mix audio separately.
- Fact-check outcome: secondary-source numbers often fail primary checks (World Bank now 2.56 bn t not 2.01; seawater 'about 40%' became IEA 'a third of capacity'); soften to what a primary source supports.

### Pacing, captions and edit
- Shots 2.5-6 s (split beats over ~6 s with two camera moves; merge beats under ~2 s into neighbours). Gentle camera moves (zoom ≤ 5%, calm shake).
- Captions: exact phrase times (segments.json), highlighted current word, held ≥ 0.7 s, placed at ~73% height, auto-shrunk to fit with 80 px side margins (a 1,175 px chunk was cropped before).

## B. Validated by our data
- **ACTIVE (2026-11-12): a hit format does not carry the next Short; choose every Short on its premise + first second, never because the format worked before.** Evidence (Studio 28 d to 11-11): WW2 all-day 1,250 vs knight 44 / pirates 12; Navy LEVELS 937 vs Egypt 24 / business 14; volcano cut-out 1,065 vs Sun 28 / hurricane 3 / dig 2. Also differed: topic, post date (Nov 9-10 Shorts only 1-3 d old), title. Source: research/short-form/2026-11-12-why-so-few-views.md.
