## 1. Ad suitability (page 6162278, Adult content)
EXPLICIT, can earn full ads:
- "Romantic scenes that aren't sexually gratifying, such as animated, real-life, or dramatized kiss or cuddling scenes."
- "discussions of romantic relationships or sexuality without reference to intercourse."
- "Fleeting implied sexual acts without visible genitals."
- Sexual jokes and innuendo, if they avoid "vulgar or obscene terms."
- Non-graphic sex education; sex-related documentaries ("documentaries about the sex industry").
- Illustrated/artistic nudity that is not arousing (sketches, computer-generated graphics).

EXPLICIT, limited ads (yellow icon):
- "Titles or thumbnails with sexualized themes (including misleading signals)."
- "Sexual innuendos, such as moaning sounds or ear nibbling"; sexual-themed pranks.
- "Sexual activities (including implied sexual acts)" (the page frames this as educational/explanatory).
- Non-arousing sex education containing animated sex acts.
- "Sex workers featured as an entity"; educational talk about personal tips learned as a sex worker.
- Dance with sustained focus on minimal clothing/body parts.

EXPLICIT, no ads:
- "Non-fleeting depictions of nudity (animated, real-life, or dramatized)."
- "Focal, implicit sexual act or behavior"; sexual acts even if blurred or implied.
- "a video thumbnail with sexual content (including texts or links)."
- "Discussions of intimate sexual experiences, such as masturbation, orgasm, intercourse, tips."
- Fetish talk, sex toys, erotic content meant to arouse.
- Page also says: "Stating comedic intent does not make highly sexualized content suitable for advertising." and the policy covers computer-generated visuals.
- Titles/thumbnails referencing adult content like "18+" are unsuitable unless educational/documentary in context.

AMBIGUOUS: the fetch gave one reading where "content related to sex industry and their workers" is no-ads, and another where "documentaries about the sex industry" are fully suitable. The page seems to split by treatment (documentary/educational vs. featuring workers as a spectacle). INTERPRETATION: sex-work history told as documentary/educational is the best case but may still be limited or yellow-iconed; any glamorizing or "tips" angle is riskier.

Sensitive events (same page): EXPLICIT "Discussions involving the loss of life or tragedy that are not exploitative or dismissive" can earn; "Creators may not monetize content that profits from or exploits a sensitive event." INTERPRETATION: scandals involving abuse, assault or deaths need a respectful tone, not jokes at victims' expense.

Note: ad suitability is judged per video by automated systems plus review; channel monetization (YPP) is separate and not yet active for us. Creators can appeal yellow icons.

## 2. Community guidelines: nudity and sexual content (page 2802002)
- EXPLICIT scope: "applies to real-world, dramatized, illustrated, and animated content." So cartoon form gives NO exemption.
- EXPLICIT prohibited: "Explicit content meant to be sexually gratifying", porn, sexual acts/fetish for gratification, nudity or partial nudity meant to gratify, non-consensual sexual content.
- EXPLICIT exception: allowed when "the primary purpose is educational, documentary, scientific, or artistic, and it isn't gratuitous." Also: "This is not a pass to promote sexually explicit content." Context in the title and description helps decide purpose.
- EXPLICIT: borderline content that is not prohibited can be age-restricted instead of removed; factors are whether sexual body parts are the focal point, intent to arouse, graphic or lewd language.
- Enforcement: warning first, then strikes; 3 strikes in 90 days can end the channel; severe cases immediate termination.

## 3. Age restriction (page 2802167)
- EXPLICIT: content not violating guidelines can still be restricted if "not appropriate for viewers under 18." Covers videos, descriptions and "custom thumbnails."
- EXPLICIT triggers (sexual): content that "invites sexual activity, such as provocative dancing or fondling"; poses meant to arouse; inappropriate clothing such as lingerie. Heavy profanity in title/thumbnail/metadata also triggers.
- Restricted videos are not viewable by under-18s or signed-out viewers, and age-restricted videos get limited or no ads (my understanding; confirm on the live page, I did not see it in the fetch). INTERPRETATION: an age restriction would cut reach for a non-monetized channel too, because signed-out/embedded viewers and recommendations to minors are cut.
- Page does not discuss animation specifically.

## 4. Thumbnails and metadata (pages 9229980, plus spam policy "malicious clickbait")
- EXPLICIT prohibited thumbnails: porn, sexual acts, nudity, "Imagery that depicts unwanted sexualization." The list is "not exhaustive." Exceptions possible for educational/documentary/scientific/artistic context.
- EXPLICIT: thumbnail that "misleads viewers to think they're about to view something that's not in the video" is not allowed.
- EXPLICIT age-restriction route: thumbnails "not appropriate for all audiences" but not violating can be age-restricted or removed with no strike; factors named are breasts, buttocks or genitals as the focal point.
- EXPLICIT (spam policy): titles, thumbnails and descriptions that trick viewers or fail to deliver what was promised can lead to monetization suspension, removal, strikes.
- EXPLICIT (ad guide): sexualized themes in title/thumbnail = limited ads; sexual content in thumbnail (including text) = no ads.
- INTERPRETATION: innuendo in a thumbnail (wink, blush, suggestive pose, "how they did it") is the gray zone. Implied nudity or focal body parts push toward age restriction or limited/no ads. Pair every cheeky promise with content that really delivers it (history, story) to avoid the misleading-metadata rule.

## 5. Animated/cartoon characters and minors (page 2801999)
- EXPLICIT: cartoons are treated like real content for sexual policy (see section 2).
- EXPLICIT (child safety): "Family friendly cartoons that target young minors and contain adult or age-inappropriate themes" are not allowed; content can't be labeled or targeted at kids (title, description, tags, audience setting) if it has adult themes.
- EXPLICIT: no content showing minors in "provocative, sexual, or sexually suggestive" situations; zero tolerance for sexualized minors.
- NOT ADDRESSED on the page I read: characters of ambiguous age. INTERPRETATION: a stickman with a round head has no clear age, which is a risk, not a shield. Make every romantic or suggestive scene clearly adult (adult context such as workplace, marriage, adult clothing, stated adult ages) and never suggestive with anyone who is or might read as a child, student or teen. Set the audience to "not made for kids" and keep the style from looking like a kids' cartoon.

## 6. Altered/synthetic content disclosure (page 14328491)
- EXPLICIT: disclosure is for realistic content: a real person saying/doing something they did not, altered real footage, realistic scenes that never happened.
- EXPLICIT: "Fantasy or clearly animated" content needs no disclosure (the page's example is a unicorn in a fantastical world); AI help with scripts, titles, captions, ideas, and cloning your own voice is exempt.
- INTERPRETATION: our stickman cartoon and Grady narrator voice (a preset voice, not a real person) should not need the label. Do not add realistic images of real people, or realistic faked scenes of real scandal figures, without ticking "AI use" in Studio. Also avoid fake quotes presented as real.

## 7. Educational/documentary treatment
- EXPLICIT: nudity/sexual policy exception for "educational, documentary, scientific, or artistic," not gratuitous. Ad guide moves sex education and sex-industry documentaries to a more permissive tier, and allows educational context for "18+/21+" wording.
- EXPLICIT limit: "not a pass to promote sexually explicit content"; clips lifted out to isolate sexual content do not count.
- INTERPRETATION: history of marriage, dating customs and sex work told as story with real context is the favorable case. Teasing the audience for sexual payoff is not.

## Main risks (summary)
1. Thumbnail is the highest-risk asset: sexualized theme = limited ads, sexual content = no ads, nudity/focal body parts = age restriction, misleading = clickbait strike.
2. Cartoon style gives no exemption (policy explicitly covers animated); even stickman nudity or implied acts are covered.
3. Sex-work topics: unclear page wording; likely limited ads at best unless clearly documentary.
4. "Tips," intimate-experience talk, fetishes, sex toys: no ads.
5. Ambiguous-age character: policy silent, so we must signal adulthood ourselves.
6. Scandals involving real people: facts only, no defamation, respectful on tragedy (not legal advice).
7. Pages were read via summarizer; the sex-industry bullets disagreed between two reads. Joe should open that section in the browser once.

## Checklist for every pillar-6 concept
1. Topic framed as history/story/education with a real point, not as arousal. Could the video be summarized without sexual content?
2. Characters clearly adult; no school, teen or child context; audience set "not made for kids".
3. Visuals: clothed, no nudity, no implied sex act on screen, no focal body parts, no lingerie, no fondling/provocative dance; cut away or use symbols (hearts, door closing).
4. Thumbnail: cheeky via expression/situation/text pun only; no body focus, no sexual words in text, no "18+/21+"; must match what the video delivers.
5. Title and description: innuendo mild, no vulgar/obscene terms or heavy profanity; no promises the video doesn't keep; no "sex tips".
6. Script: no graphic or first-person intimate detail, no fetish content, no "how to" sexual advice; sex-work subjects told historically/documentary, not glamorized.
7. Scandals: sourced facts, no unproven accusations, respectful of victims and deaths.
8. No realistic AI depictions of real people; if any, tick "AI use" disclosure.
9. Expect possible yellow icon; do not treat it as failure. Plan an age-restriction fallback (edit and re-upload is better than appeal if the video can't be cleaned).
10. Before publishing: re-open the live policy pages if more than 90 days old; log outcome (ad status, age restriction) in learnings.md for each pillar-6 video.
