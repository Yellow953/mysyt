## FIVE CROSS-VIDEO LESSONS ON PREMISE STRENGTH (what a story must pass to be clickable)
1. A universal "I've always wondered" question beats a clever angle on a niche. (OBSERVED: Omini's same-format niche what-ifs get 3-7K vs 1.1M; Ink's WW2 siblings on lesser wars 8-27K vs 493K; Relic's Toilet 278K vs 1-2K on non-body topics. The premise's baseline demand did the work.) For us: a story needs a question a stranger already asks (a world-famous event, object, or human wonder) or a hook that makes them ask it in one line.
2. The promise must contain a contrast/inversion the viewer can feel in four words. (OBSERVED in titles/thumbs: "Actually" + NO FIGHTING; TODAY vs 50 YEARS LATER; NO TOILETS?; $8 a head vs owner.) It is the gap between what they assume and what the title implies. HYPOTHESIS that this gap is what drives CTR; no CTR data seen.
3. The thumbnail promises one concrete image, not the topic. (OBSERVED: a feral dog for "everyone disappears", a soldier napping for WW2, shocked cave people for toilets; comments name the thumbnail twice on Toilet.) Test: can the premise be drawn as one funny/alarming/sad picture with at most 2 words? If not, it is a weak click.
4. The premise carries a built-in payoff the viewer can name before watching: a loop or a final state. (OBSERVED endings: Ink loops back to the rifle on the boot; Relic returns to the bush line; Ranks returns to a "for lease" sign with 9,100 names; Arnie flips so the hero answers the door in his socks; Omini ends on "it's a job".) HYPOTHESIS: viewers stay for the return, so a story must have a "start state" and a changed "end state" decided before drafting.
5. Stakes must be a price tag or a clock, not a theme. (OBSERVED: "$8 a head", "$41 in checking", "$4.60 vs $3.14 a bushel", "10,000 planes", "1596 / 1775", "first 10 seconds". Every video states a number inside the first 30 s or the first scene.) HYPOTHESIS: concrete numbers make an abstract premise feel verifiable and give the viewer something to carry; an idea with no number or date in the first line is too soft.

Honest limit: all five videos are single samples of 3 windows each; "why viewers stay" is OBSERVED only where tied to something I read (captions, comments); there is no retention data, so everything about retention curves is hypothesis. Comments on Ink Story look partly bot-like. Thumbnails were seen via the image URL, partly cut by the screenshot overlay.
