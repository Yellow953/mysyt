# Outline: What happens if you swallow gum? (2026-11-09-swallow-gum) STOP for Joe

## Honest check
The question is universal and everyone "knows" the answer wrong, so the click is easy. But the truth is one flat fact ("it leaves like anything else"), so a plain myth-bust is thin. The story has to be the mascot's seven-year bet, and the payoff a surprise: gum is as indigestible as corn, and your body just walks it out. Strong enough as a 35 s Short if the calendar gag carries the middle and the reveal is quick. Weakest part: no emotional stake beyond the gag. Fallback stronger angle: "Gum is basically corn" (visual, new to most). It is built in as the twist.

## Central question
Does a swallowed piece of gum really sit in your stomach for seven years?

## Premise / stake
Mr. Yellow Scarf swallows a piece by accident, believes the myth, and starts a seven-year countdown. Stake: he thinks he is now storing gum. Lazy everyman sits and waits.

## Conflict / turn
Myth (it stays, seven years) vs truth (your body cannot digest gum base, so it does what it does with corn: moves it along and out within days). Turn: the thing he fears is the exact reason it leaves.

## Hook + frame-0 options (plain Grady wording, no "!")
1. Whole illustrated image at frame 0: him wide-eyed with a gum wad mid-gulp, calendar "7 YEARS" behind. Line: "You swallowed gum. It lives in you for seven years." (RECOMMENDED; premise in one line, punch on "seven")
2. "Swallow gum, and it sits in your stomach for seven years. Everyone knows that." Punch on "Everyone knows".
3. Calendar already marked seven years, him frozen: "He swallowed one piece of gum, and started a seven-year countdown."

## Beats (~35-40 s, ~75-85 words)
1. HOOK (0-4 s). WHOLE image (hook) + line. Earns the swipe stop.
2. Everyone heard it (4-9 s, cut-out). He gulps, gets a calendar, circles day one. Sets the bet; "everyone has heard this since childhood".
3. Why the myth feels right (9-16 s, cut-out). Gum base is indigestible, no enzyme in you can break it down (Duke). Show enzyme scissors failing / gum bouncing. Lets the myth sound true so the twist lands.
4. The turn (16-24 s, cut-out). Indigestible is not the same as stuck. Same reason corn kernels and popcorn husks leave: your gut just pushes it along. Show gum riding the conveyor with a corn kernel. Pays the hook's fear.
5. Day 2-3 reveal (24-31 s, cut-out). Calendar page flips, it is gone within days (hedged). Doctors who look in stomachs do not find old wads.
6. Caveat (31-35 s, cut-out). Only real trouble: kids swallowing lots of gum over time, with other problems. Not one piece. Honest hedge, keeps trust.
7. Ending (35-40 s). See below.

Cut-out animation: beats 2-6. WHOLE illustrated image: beat 1 (hook, doubles as cover) and optionally the day-3 reveal calendar if budget allows (Joe's rule: at least one).

## Endings (recommend 1)
1. RECOMMENDED loop: he tears the calendar, shrugs "so I waited for nothing", then pops a new gum in; cut to frame 0 image idea (gulp). Pays off the hook and loops. Line: "So the seven years were a lie. Anyway, I have another piece."
2. Corn twist: "Gum is basically corn. Don't ask what your calendar was for."
3. Dry sign-off: "Seven years of waiting. Gone in three days. Worst deal ever, honestly."

## Emotional journey
Alarm (hook) -> smug dread (calendar) -> "oh no, it is truly stuck" -> relief and laugh (it leaves like corn) -> trust (honest caveat) -> loop.

## Creative choices

- Corn comparison (Duke) is the sourced, visual twist.
- Medical-advice note goes in the description, not the narration.
