# Research: What if you swallowed a piece of gum? (2026-11-09)
Verified 2026-11-09 via web search + page fetches. Mayo Clinic pages themselves returned 403 / could not be opened here; Mayo statements are cited via the Mayo news network summary and secondary reports quoting Mayo doctors, so they are marked probable where only secondary. PubMed also blocked (cookies); the 1998 Pediatrics report was confirmed through Scientific American's description.

## Claims table
| Claim | Source | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Swallowed gum stays in your stomach for seven years" is false | Mayo Clinic Minute (Dr. Mark Larson: "That simply is not true"); Duke Health (Dr. McGreal); Sci Am | VERIFIED | Mayo video found via search summary; Duke + Sci Am pages read directly |
| Gum base is indigestible; the body has no enzyme to break it down | Duke Health blog, Dr. McGreal: gum base "insoluble, just like the fiber base of raw vegetables, corn, popcorn kernels, and seeds"; "Our bodies do not possess digestive enzymes to specifically break down gum base" | VERIFIED | Sci Am: base "largely indigestible"; sweeteners and flavor DO get broken down |
| Indigestible does not mean it stays; it moves along with food and leaves in stool | Duke Health: "simply travels the same path as food and is excreted in stool" | VERIFIED | |
| Stomach empties 30-120 min after eating, gum included | Duke Health | VERIFIED | Not needed in narration |
| Passes in "about a day or two" | WTAMU Q&A quoting Dr. Michael Picco (Mayo); other sources 1-3 days, ~40 h | PROBABLE | Secondary, ranges vary. Narration: "within days" (hedged) |
| Doctors do not find wads of gum sitting in stomachs at endoscopy | Duke, Dr. McGreal: "I have yet to see a wad of gum lying around in the stomach"; Sci Am (Milov: swallowed gum seen is "not... more than a week old") | VERIFIED (as one doctor's report) | Say "doctors who look inside stomachs", not "all doctors" |
| Origin of the "seven years" number | Sci Am: "unknown origin" ; Duke: "may trace to generations of parents" warning kids | DISPUTED / UNKNOWN | Do NOT state an origin as fact. "Nobody knows where seven came from" is safe (Sci Am). Parent-warning idea = speculation, only say "probably parents" as a joke/hedged |
| Rare harm: blockages from gum | Pediatrics 1998 "Chewing gum bezoars of the gastrointestinal tract" (3 children), via Sci Am; Mayo (Rajan, via secondary): a child with constipation eating a very large amount can contribute to a blockage | VERIFIED (rare, specific cases) | In the 1998 report two kids swallowed gum habitually (given as rewards) and got constipated gum masses; third (18 months) had gum fusing coins in the esophagus. Pattern: repeated/large amounts or other swallowed objects, mostly children |
| One piece = harmless | Mayo (via secondary: "generally isn't harmful if swallowed"); Duke; Sci Am | PROBABLE-to-VERIFIED | Hedge: "one piece is fine" is the consensus, but we are not giving medical advice; description note |
| Sugar-free gum sweeteners in large quantity can cause nausea/diarrhea | Duke | VERIFIED | Optional, not in narration |
| "Seven years" myth is nearly universally known | Sci Am ("almost universal renown") | VERIFIED | Supports the hook ("everyone heard this") |
