## Facts & sources
1. Fur loss: a genetics-based estimate (skin pigmentation) suggests about 1.2-2 million years ago; leading ideas: cooling by sweat, fewer parasites (Pagel); timing and reasons debated, hence "Scientists still argue why" and "Maybe". https://www.discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/why-humans-lost-their-hair-and-became-naked-and-sweaty · https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/latest-theory-human-body-hair
2. "Then winter happened.": joke; Gilligan links clothing to cold climate swings (thermal model). https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/the-prehistoric-development-of-clothing-archaeological-implicatio/
3. Hides: use-wear on stone scrapers from Hoxne (England) and Qesem shows hide preparation about 400,000 years ago; no direct evidence earlier. This is hide-working, not proof of clothing; the script says "scraping hides". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_hide_materials
4. Lice: clothing lice split from head lice at least ~83,000 and possibly ~170,000 years ago (Toups et al. 2011); "hints ... by about a hundred thousand" is inside that range; a louse split date, not a direct clothing date; critics: the split could come after clothing began. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3002236
5. Needles: Gilligan et al., Science Advances, 2024: eyed needles (Siberia, ~40,000 years ago) allowed fitted, layered, decorated clothing; the authors' interpretation, hence "A 2024 study links". https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2024/06/29/the-beginnings-of-fashion.html · https://archaeology.org/issues/september-october-2024/artifacts/paleolithic-eyed-needles/
6. "Survival gear became a statement": Gilligan: from a physical necessity in cold places to a social necessity in all environments. Same source.
7. Not used (unverified or contested): "only ape that wears clothes", bead dates, modesty as an origin, any "first clothing" year.
