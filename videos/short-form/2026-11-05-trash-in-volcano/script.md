# Why don't we throw our trash in a volcano? (cut-out + Remotion pilot, H8)
Status: PRODUCED 2026-11-05 (final wording below). Joe approved + asked to make it more interesting. (Sun version shelved in ../2026-11-05-trash-at-the-sun/.)
Originality: same QUESTION as Danny Why's reference video (allowed), but our own answer (heat is not the problem; no filter is) and our own gags; NOT the "volcano spits the bag back" gag.

## Script (~55 words, est. ~27 s with Grady)
1. Why don't we just throw our trash in a volcano?
2. Lava is over a thousand degrees, so it would burn it, easy.
3. But only a handful of volcanoes keep an open lava lake.
4. And burning trash makes toxic stuff, like dioxins and heavy metals.
5. Incinerators catch it with filters. A volcano is just an incinerator without one.

## Claims (verified 2026-11-05 by web search)
| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Basalt lava erupts at ~1,100-1,250 C (Kilauea ~1,170 C) | VERIFIED (USGS) | usgs.gov Kilauea FAQ; usgs.gov "How hot is a Hawaiian volcano" |
| Earth has only a handful of long-lived lava lakes (Erebus, Erta Ale, Nyiragongo among them; Kilauea's disappeared 2018) | VERIFIED (USGS Volcano Watch "Earth has just a handful of long-lived lava lakes"); no single official current count, so wording stays "a handful" | usgs.gov/center-news/earth-has-just-a-handful-long-lived-lava-lakes |
| Waste incineration is a significant source of dioxins and heavy metals (lead, cadmium, mercury); plants use scrubbers, fabric filters, activated carbon; EPA has set limits since the 1990s | VERIFIED (review papers + EPA/OSTI); "nothing to catch them" for a volcano is our logical step, not a measured claim | osti.gov/biblio/353613; epa.gov municipal waste combustor rules |
| "Heat is not the problem": lava is hotter than the 850 C+ incinerators run at | not stated in the script; avoid the incinerator temperature number unless sourced | |
Not claimed: that trash would explode or change eruptions; that volcanoes are unreachable (not verified).
