In 1932, Australia went to war with emus. Soldiers. Machine guns. Ten thousand bullets. And the farmers paid for the bullets. The gun jammed. A politician said the emus deserved medals. The army pulled out. By the headlines, Australia lost a war to birds. So the farmers took over. The government paid a bounty for every beak. One count says over fifty-seven thousand emus, in a single season. The army managed under a thousand, by its own count. The winning weapon wasn't a machine gun. It was a payment per beak.
