# You Wake Up and the Sun Is Gone

## Idea
- **Angle:** second-person thought experiment; the twist is that you would not notice for ~8 minutes. Lazy everyman eating breakfast.
- **Triggers:** curiosity gap, extremes, self-insertion.
- **Hook:** "You wake up and the Sun is gone. Funny thing. It's about ninety-three million miles away, so nobody notices for eight minutes." Spoiler + twist inside ~8 s; the open loop is "what happens then?".

## Script (phrases)
You wake up and the Sun is gone.
Funny thing. It's about ninety-three million miles away, so nobody notices for eight minutes.
That's how long sunlight takes to reach us.
So you eat breakfast under a perfectly bright sky.
Gravity is just as slow. So Earth keeps circling a Sun that isn't there,
like a dog chasing a ball you only pretended to throw.
Then, at the exact same moment, the sky goes black and Earth lets go.
It flies off in a straight line.
Then it gets cold. Your coffee goes first.
What would you do with your last eight minutes?

## Facts & sources
1. Light takes about 8 min 20 s from the Sun to Earth; gravity's effect also spreads at light speed, so Earth keeps its orbit for the same window. https://www.astronomy.com/?p=43150 (Marc Kuchner, NASA Goddard) · https://wtamu.edu/~cbaird/sq/2013/04/30/what-is-the-speed-of-gravity/
2. After that Earth, no longer pulled toward the Sun, would move off in a straight line (Newton's first law; the same sources).
3. Relativity does not allow the Sun to vanish instantly (mass cannot disappear); this stays in the description only. Narration treats it as a pure thought experiment (Joe: the fun is imagining the outcome).
5. "Then it gets cold": without sunlight the surface would cool (imagined outcome; the coffee line is a joke).
4. "Eight minutes" is rounded from 8 min 20 s.
