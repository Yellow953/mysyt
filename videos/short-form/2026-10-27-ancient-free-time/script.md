## Facts & sources
1. Foragers' time: Richard Lee's Ju/'hoansi (!Kung): about 12-19 h/week of food-getting (a few hours a day); adding processing, tool making and housework about 40-44 h/week, hence "Chores came extra." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Borshay_Lee · https://quillette.com/2017/12/16/romanticizing-hunter-gatherer/
2. Wiessner 2014 (PNAS, "Embers of society: Firelight talk among the Ju/'hoansi Bushmen"): 174 day and night conversations recorded in 1974 (five or more people); day talk centered on economic matters and gossip/norm regulation; at night 81% of lengthy many-person conversations were stories, often about known people (a PNAS commentary: "more than 80%"). Spoken as "81% of long chats". https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4191796 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4191795 · https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/09/140922152809.htm
3. "So you nap. Fix a spear. Gossip. Your hobby: other people.": resting, visiting and tool-making are standard categories of forager time use; gossip is from Wiessner's day talk. Joke-level summary.
4. "Modern Kalahari foragers" and "Not cavemen" make clear these are not Paleolithic humans.
5. "Maybe boredom built culture" = Wiessner's hypothesis that firelight talk helped build culture, hedged with "maybe". "Your group chat is basically a campfire" is a joke, not a claim.
6. Not used (unverified): the haiku analysis's "1963 researcher", "2008 study", "90,000 working hours".
