# Research: What if you stopped blinking for a day? (Stage D, 2026-11-09)
Limits of this pass: AAO, Mayo, NEI and PubMed pages were blocked or timed out for automated fetch (cookie wall, socket errors). Claims below rest on search-result excerpts of reliable sources; anything not read at the primary page is marked probable and hedged in narration. Nothing here is medical advice.

## Claims table
| Claim | Source | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal blink rate is roughly 15 per minute | AAO news release (Nov 2023) as reported by HealthDay; Sheedy/Ohio State baseline ~15/min (CBS, Fox); Portello 2013 (PubMed 24413278): 13.6-14.9/min | verified (range) | Reviews range 8-21 /min (10-16 in one PMC review). Say "around fifteen a minute", NOT "15-20". |
| Screens cut blinking about in half | AAO via HealthDay | disputed | Portello 2013 found NO significant drop in rate on screens (14.9 vs 13.6) but significantly more INCOMPLETE blinks. Safe: "you blink less completely when staring at a screen" or drop. |
| Blink spreads the tear film over the eye, keeping it moist | Clinical Ophthalmology review (dovepress, screen use and dry eye); PMC tear-film literature | verified | Tears = water, mucin, lipids. |
| Blinking helps clear particles/debris | Live Science quoting an AAO spokesperson; ophthalmology teaching material | probable | Primary page not read. Narration: "wipes away dust". |
| Tear film breaks up and that discomfort triggers the next blink | tear-film physiology sources | probable | Mechanism simplified; hedge ("your eye tells you to blink"). |
| Blinking is largely a reflex, but you can blink on purpose or hold it for a while | Live Science; physiology sources (spontaneous / reflex / voluntary) | verified | Do NOT claim it is impossible to suppress for a few seconds. |
| Maximum blink interval (how long people can hold eyes open) correlates with tear film breakup time; shorter in dry-eye patients | Sci Rep 2018 (PMC6128834): 292 patients; MBI 10.0 +- 9.1 s dry eye vs 24.3 +- 38.2 s non-dry-eye; cutoff 12.4 s | verified (patients, not healthy volunteers) | Huge spread. | Eyes cannot be held open for a full day | No source states this directly | hypothetical | Do not state as fact. Say "nobody has come close" only via the record below, or frame as the imagined answer. |
| Dry eye symptoms (irritation, blurry vision) follow poor tear film | dry-eye literature (Clin Ophthalmol review) | verified | Blur/irritation are standard dry-eye symptoms; do not claim permanent damage. |

## Notes
- Honest answer to the question: you cannot do it. The real story is "your eyes force you to blink; the longest people have gone is about an hour, with effort".
- No evidence for harm beyond dry, irritated, blurry eyes; avoid any "you'd go blind" line.
- Idea is interesting without jokes: it is a thing the viewer can try right now (participation hook).
- Strongest hook found: the viewer is already part of the experiment ("Don't blink").
