## Facts (light check; jokes exaggerate)
Infantrymen spent much of their time digging foxholes, cleaning gear, carrying supplies, standing watch and waiting (dawn stand-to; see Ink Story's "What Did WW2 Soldiers Actually Do All Day?" and war-museum routine pages); K-rations came in boxes with canned meat, biscuits and gum; trench foot from wet feet hit tens of thousands of US infantrymen in 1944-45, so dry socks mattered; mail from home (V-mail) was a big morale item; hot meals when units could get them. Sources: https://www.normandywarguide.com/articles/ww2-american-k-ration-dinner · https://dp.la/exhibitions/home-front-world-war-ii/soldiers-families · https://www.warmuseum.ca/firstworldwar/history/life-at-the-front/trench-conditions/trench-routine/ (WWI routine; stand-to).
