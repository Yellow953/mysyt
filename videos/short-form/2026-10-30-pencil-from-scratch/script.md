## Script (phrases)
You have a forest and a mine. Build one pencil.
Easy. It's wood and a stick of lead.
There is no lead. It's graphite and clay.
A famous 1958 essay traced one pencil around the world.
Graphite from Ceylon. Clay from Mississippi.
Cedar from California and Oregon. You brought a pickaxe to a tree problem.
The metal band is brass. That's two more mines. You have one.
Six coats of lacquer. Castor beans are involved. Of course.
The essay's point: not one person on Earth knows how to make a pencil.
You, with your forest and your mine? Good luck.
Could you make one?

## Facts & sources (all from I, Pencil unless noted)
1. https://fee.org/articles/i-pencil/ : cedar from Northern California and Oregon; graphite mined in Ceylon ("lead" contains no lead); clay from Mississippi mixed with graphite; brass from zinc and copper; eraser is factice from rapeseed oil (Dutch East Indies) with pumice from Italy; six coats of lacquer incl. castor oil; "not a single person on the face of this earth" knows how to make it.
2. Modern pencils differ (other woods, sources and materials; ferrules may be aluminum); the narration says "a famous 1958 essay tracked it". Source for modern variations: https://www.encyclopedia.com/literature-and-arts/art-and-architecture/art-general/pencil
3. Graphite + clay core: encyclopedia.com above.
4. "Two more mines": the essay says brass is made from zinc and copper "mined from nature". Worded as the essay's account.
