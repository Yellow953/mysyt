# Why can't we just drink seawater? (cut-out batch 3/4, H8) - ~59 s version
Status: PRODUCED 2026-11-08 (waiting on Joe's verdict). Credits ~7.3 (see cost.md).

## Final recorded script (Grady, 11 clips, sped atempo 1.10; total 59.3 s with 0.7 s loop tail)
1. Why can't we just drink seawater?
2. There's water everywhere. Free refills.
3. But seawater has over three times the salt of your blood.
4. Your kidneys can't make pee saltier than the sea, so flushing the salt costs more water than you drank.
5. Congratulations. You're thirstier.
6. The Gulf is hot desert with almost no rain, so it uses the sea anyway.
7. Desalination plants boil seawater and catch the steam, or push it through filters too fine for salt.
8. One Saudi plant makes a million cubic meters a day. About four hundred Olympic pools.
9. The Gulf holds a third of the world's desalination capacity. Kuwait relies on it almost completely.
10. The catch? It burns lots of energy, and the leftover brine can hurt the sea.
11. So yes, you can drink the sea. You just need a very big plant.

## Claims (verified 2026-11-08)
| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Seawater has over 3x the salt of human blood | SUPPORTED: ocean ~3.5% salt (35 g/kg, DOSITS/Britannica 34-37 ppt); blood plasma ~0.9% (isotonic saline 0.9%, NIH/PMC); ratio ~3.9x, so "over three times" is safe. No NOAA/USGS page states the blood comparison directly. | NIH PMC4794509, DOSITS salinity glossary |
| Kidneys cannot make urine saltier than seawater; flushing salt costs more water than drunk | VERIFIED (primary) | NOAA National Ocean Service, oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/drinksw.html: "Human kidneys can only make urine that is less salty than salt water." |
| Gulf (GCC) holds ~a third of world desalination CAPACITY | VERIFIED (one official source, metric = installed capacity, 33%) | IEA commentary "Wired for water: how electrification is transforming desalination" (GCC alone 33%; MENA over 40%) |
| Kuwait relies almost completely on desalination | SUPPORTED, wording softened | IEA (same page: "Kuwait ... relying almost exclusively on desalination"); news reports ~90% of drinking water (SCMP 2026, secondary; Borgen/UN 2019 ~93%) not used as a number |
| Ras Al-Khair (Saudi) ~1.03 million m3/day, hybrid thermal + RO | VERIFIED capacity (Bloomberg 2014: 1.025 million m3/day design; Guinness 2016 as 1,036,000 m3/day per Saudipedia). Script says "a million cubic meters" and does not say "largest". | Bloomberg, Saudipedia, Arab Business |
| 1.03M m3 / 2,500 m3 per Olympic pool = ~410 | OUR ARITHMETIC; script says "about four hundred" | |
| Two methods: thermal (boil/flash, catch steam) and reverse osmosis (filter that blocks salt) | VERIFIED (IEA: SWRO 2.5-6 kWh/m3, thermal more energy; Ras Al-Khair is MSF + RO) | IEA |
| Desal needs lots of energy; brine can hurt the sea | VERIFIED | IEA ("energy use ... up to 15% of total final consumption"; "concentrated brine can damage ecosystems if poorly handled"); UN World Ocean Assessment ch. 8 (brine discharge raises salinity/temperature) |
| Gulf is hot desert with almost no rain | VERIFIED | UN World Ocean Assessment ch. 28 (GCC arid, avg rainfall 75-140 mm/year) |
