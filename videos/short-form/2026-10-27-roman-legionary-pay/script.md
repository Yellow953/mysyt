## Facts & sources
1. 225 denarii a year under Augustus (Caesar doubled it); paid in three instalments of 75; Domitian raised it to 300 (end of 1st c.). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_(Roman_army) · https://erenow.org/ancient/the-complete-roman-army/14.php (Goldsworthy, The Complete Roman Army)
2. Deductions: food, boots/straps, clothing, hay appear in Egyptian papyri; one record shows 80 drachmas for food out of 247.5 received in the first instalment, about 32%, hence "about a third". https://erenow.org/ancient/the-complete-roman-army/14.php (Goldsworthy). Instalment count (three) from Goldsworthy only; Wikipedia gives no deduction figures.
3. Soldiers paid for clothing, weapons and tents (Tacitus, mutiny of AD 14). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_(Roman_army)
4. Hay/mule: NOT attested as a mule deduction; the source only speculates hay was for bedding or the squad's mule, so the script says "maybe". Tent: Tacitus reports soldiers' complaints about paying for clothes, weapons and tents, so "griped". https://erenow.org/ancient/the-complete-roman-army/14.php
5. Discharge: praemia after a term of 25 years or more: 3,000 denarii from Augustus (rose to 5,000 under Caracalla) and/or farmland. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_legion · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_(Roman_army). 3000/225 = 13.3 years at starting pay, so "about thirteen years of starting pay".
6. **Hedged/dropped:** "burial fund" deduction DROPPED (attested only in an AD 192 Egyptian auxiliary record; Wikipedia and Goldsworthy excerpt do not list it). Masada payslip DROPPED (soldier's unit disputed: auxiliary vs legionary). No amounts claimed for boots/tent. "Domitian's raise" worded "raised it", not a fraction (sources say one-third or one-quarter; 225 to 300 is one-third). Some deductions were refunded when kit was bought back at retirement or death; "buy your own mule" is a joke (the mule was the squad's). Pay rates varied by period, unit and rank (a centurion earned far more).
