# The Internet Dies. What Still Works?

## Idea
- **Angle:** "You wake up and the internet is gone." The payoff is a practical list of what still works, plus one honest bad-news line (undersea cables).
- **Triggers:** curiosity gap, self-insertion, utility/saveable (people save checklists).

## Script (phrases)
You wake up. The whole world's internet is gone.
No email. No streaming. No cat videos. Your boss can't reach you, though.
Your phone feels like a brick. It isn't.
GPS still works. Those satellites don't need the internet.
Saved a map earlier? It still guides you home. Nobody saved a map earlier.
An old battery radio still plays the news and emergency alerts. Yes, the one in the drawer.
Calls and texts? Maybe. Depends on your carrier.
The bad news: almost all data between continents runs through undersea cables. The internet is basically wet.
So, what would you do first?

## Facts & sources
1. GPS works without internet or cell data (receivers use satellite signals; assisted GPS is only faster at the first fix). https://gisgeography.com/can-gps-work-without-the-internet/ · https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt/blog/does-gps-work-without-data
2. Offline maps work if downloaded beforehand (static, no live traffic). Same sources.
3. Battery or hand-crank AM/FM radios work when internet and cell networks are down; local stations carry Emergency Alert System messages. https://poweroutage.us/storm-prep/information-during-power-outage · Craig Fugate (former FEMA head) via https://hurricanesafety.org/tag/apple/
4. Some phones can receive FM with headphones as the antenna: FCC filing https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-333231A2.pdf (many phones have the chip disabled, so "some").
5. Calls and texts depend on the carrier and network congestion; stated as "maybe". (Fugate: cell networks go down or get congested in disasters.)
6. Undersea cables carry over 99% of intercontinental data traffic (TeleGeography confirms, noting satellite traffic is not precisely measured): https://resources.telegeography.com/2023-mythbusting-part-3 . Narration says "almost all".
7. This is a thought experiment; a total global internet outage is not expected.
