## Facts & sources
1. Foraging time: Richard Lee's Ju/'hoansi (!Kung): about 12–19 hours/week of food-getting; adding food processing, tool making and housework, Lee estimated about 40–44 hours/week. Diamond cites 12–19 h for Kalahari Bushmen and ~14 h for the Hadza. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Borshay_Lee · https://quillette.com/2017/12/16/romanticizing-hunter-gatherer/ · https://hettingern.people.charleston.edu/150_Spring_2015/Diamond_Worst_Mistake.htm
2. Caveat: low figures cover food-getting only; critics note they ignore difficulty and danger. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_affluent_society
3. Heights: skeletons from Greece and Turkey, men ~5'9" at the end of the ice ages vs ~5'3" later (Diamond; copies differ on the date, 300 BC vs 3000 BC, so no date is given). Worded "suggest" because interpretation (selection vs real decline) is debated. https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-worst-mistake-in-the-history-of-the-human-race-12157
4. Disease: Diamond: crowded farming populations spread infectious diseases and parasites; foragers had no epidemics. Same essay.
5. Crop failure: single-crop risk (Diamond cites the Irish Potato Famine, not named in the Short). Same essay.
6. Stored grain and bosses: Diamond: foragers stored no food, so no elite class; stored food let an elite seize food and rule. "First boss" is a joke built on this. Same essay.
7. "One famous scientist called farming the worst mistake in human history": Jared Diamond, Discover, 1987 (credit him in the description).
8. "Some of your ancestors" (not "all"): foraging time varied by group and season. "Usually no boss": many foraging bands were egalitarian, not all.
9. Farming began roughly 10,000–12,000 years ago (the Short no longer states a year).
10. "You still work forty": a standard full-time week is about 40 hours (joke-level, typical not universal).
