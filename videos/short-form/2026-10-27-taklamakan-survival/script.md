## Facts & sources
1. **Size:** about 337,000 km2, slightly smaller than Germany (not used in the narration). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taklamakan_Desert
2. **Name:** folk etymology "place of no return / get in and you'll never get out"; researchers agree "makan" is Arabic for "place", the origin of "takla" is unclear. Hence "often said". Same source.
3. **Rain:** the Tazhong station in the middle averages 25.8 mm (1.03 in) of precipitation a year. HEDGE: ONE station's average, so the script says "One weather station in the middle". Same source.
4. **Dunes move:** moving dunes bury farmland and homes; sandstorms are likely and dangerous. Same source.
5. **Temperatures:** summers can reach about 40 C (104 F; station record 45.6 C); winters sometimes fall well below -20 C (-4 F). Script: "can top a hundred degrees ... can fall below zero" (Fahrenheit-feel for the US audience; both are "can"). Same source.
6. **Bones as trail markers:** Faxian (set out 399 CE, wrote his account around 414) describes the desert west of Dunhuang with "many evil demons and hot winds", "not a bird ... nor an animal", and only "the dry bones of the dead" as guides. HEDGE: that is the Gobi/Lop stretch east of the Taklamakan, hence "a monk crossing next door" and no date. https://depts.washington.edu/silkroad/texts/faxian.html · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faxian
7. **Marco Polo's spirits:** in the Desert of Lop a straggler hears spirits that talk like his comrades and "call him by name"; caravans kept close, with bells on the animals (Yule translation). HEDGE: Lop is the neighbouring stretch at the Taklamakan's eastern end, and the book was dictated to Rustichello, a medieval traveller's tale; the script says "Marco Polo's book says". https://librivox.org/the-book-of--marco-polo-1-by-rustichello-da-pisa/ · https://dsr.nii.ac.jp/toyobunko/VIII-5-B2-9/V-2/page/0028.html.en
8. **Edge/oases:** merchant caravans stopped at thriving oasis towns around the rim (Kashgar, Yarkand, Khotan, Niya, Kuqa, Turpan, Loulan, Dunhuang) on the northern and southern routes. Wikipedia source above.
9. **Camels:** camels were the usual caravan animal, carried heavy loads and could go several days without water (the "go days without" claim was cut from the narration; the camel line is only "carried the water"). https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/caravan/605276 · https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/marco-polo-in-china-archival
10. **Dropped (unverified or contested):** Xuanzang's lost-water episode (Gashun Gobi, not the Taklamakan); Sven Hedin 1895 deaths (his dramatized account); exact oasis spacing and crossing-day counts; hired-guide practices (the guide appears only as image colour, never in the narration); dune heights (~18-91 m, verified but cut for time).
