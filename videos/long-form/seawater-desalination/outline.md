# Outline: Why can't you just drink the sea? (long-form, ~5 min)
Story Architect, 2026-11-08. Expands the approved Short `videos/short-form/2026-11-08-drink-seawater/script.md` into a ~300 s story. No script yet (script comes after Joe OKs this outline).

Claim source: `videos/long-form/seawater-desalination/research.md` only. Where the Short's claim check and research.md disagree, research.md wins (see Conflicts below).

Marks used on every beat:
- [verified] = research.md status VERIFIED or SUPPORTED, used with its safe wording.
- [hedged] = used only with an honest-uncertainty line (research.md section N) or a "by one source / estimates vary" qualifier.
- [needs check] = not in research.md, or the research row is shaky; the fact-checker must resolve before the script uses it.
- [dramatization] = imagined scene or dialogue, never presented as fact.

---

## 1. Premise

You are stranded on a rock with an ocean in every direction and a cup of seawater in your hand. The sea looks like the answer. It isn't, and the reason is inside your body. Then the story moves to the Gulf, where whole countries have no rivers to drink from and have learned to make fresh water out of the sea, paying for it with oil money, fuel and a fragile intake pipe. The cold open question is answered at the end: yes, you can drink the sea, but only with a very big plant, and the sea fights back.

Narrator: "you" only. No name for the mascot, no named partner or narrator. Scarf stays yellow.

Story arc (two halves, each with its own mini-arc):
- Body half (0:00 to 1:20): want = a drink. Attempt = sip the sea. Obstacle = salt, kidneys, thirst. Turn = even small sips fail, so "then how do people get fresh water?"
- Gulf half (1:20 to 4:40): want = fresh water for a place with no rivers. Attempt 1 = boats from Iraq. Attempt 2 = oil-fired boiling and filter plants. Obstacles = energy, brine, algae bloom, oil slick. Payoff = the plants run, but only as a fragile, costly system.
- Ending (4:40 to 5:00): pays off the cold open with the same cup.

## 2. Hook options (first 8 s, the one picture at frame 0)

**H1 (recommended): castaway cold open.**
- Picture at frame 0: whole illustrated image of the mascot on a tiny rock island, cup in hand, huge ocean, dramatic sun. Zoom punch on the question.
- Line: "Stranded. Ocean in every direction. A cup of it in your hand. Why can't you just drink the sea?"
- Why: instant premise, the question is the title, and the ending can pay off the same island and cup. Matches Joe's brief.
- Trade-off: Short already uses this image; fine for long-form because it is the cold open for the full story, but the Short's approved line 2 ("Free refills") should be the sip gag, not the hook.

**H2: the 1943 lab test.**
- Picture: illustrated 1943 lab with a row of volunteers and a stopwatch.
- Line: "In 1943, scientists tested whether a few sips of seawater could keep a person alive. Here is what they found."
- Why: real human experiment, strong curiosity gap, feels like a documentary.
- Trade-off: the research is only a snippet (Ladell full text not read, research M). The payoff lands at about 1:20, which is slow for a hook. Needs the full text before it can be the promise.

**H3: the 1991 oil slick.**
- Picture: whole illustrated black slick sliding toward a row of intake pipes, workers with booms.
- Line: "In 1991, a black oil slick was heading for the pipes that bring a Gulf city its water."
- Why: the most dramatic stakes in the research (H5 VERIFIED threat).
- Trade-off: the payoff comes at about 4:15, which is far too early to spend the best image. Also the story needs the body and Gulf context first. Keep it for the act-3 climax, not the hook.

## 3. Ending options

**E1 (recommended): island callback.**
- The mascot is back on the rock with the same cup of sea and a dented pot. He boils the cup over a small fire, lid catches the steam, a drip falls into his mouth. The salt stays in the pot.
- Voiceover beat: "Boil the sea, catch the steam, leave the salt behind. The big plants do exactly that, just with a fuel bill." Then one line of CTA, in character.
- CTA (one line): "Subscribe for the next one. Just don't drink the sea."
- Why: pays off the cold open literally, uses the verified boil method, ends on a visual gag.
- Trade-off: needs care that the home version is an illustration of the principle only. No claim that it is safe or efficient. [needs check] for any "works at home" implication; keep it as a gag, not advice.

**E2: the dhow boatman callback.**
- The dhow from beat 5 comes back. The boatman is selling fresh water from Iraq at a price. You offer your cup of seawater. He laughs. The cup stays on the boat rail.
- CTA: "Subscribe for the next one. The sea is free, but the boat is not."
- Why: ties the Gulf half back to a human story and gives the boat beat its payoff.
- Trade-off: the cold-open answer ("you can drink the sea, with a very big plant") is less visible; the island is dropped, which weakens the arc. Use if Joe wants a Gulf-only close.

## 6. What keeps the viewer watching at each lull

- Beats 2 to 4 (body half, explanation risk): each beat ends on a new question. Beat 2 ends with "what does salt do to you?", beat 3 with "why does the body charge water?", beat 4 with "how do people get fresh water?". The gag in each beat (spit, shrug, stopwatch) keeps the tone light.
- Beat 9 (dependence numbers, risk of a stats pile): keep it to two numbers at most and make it visual (gauge dropping). Ends on "what can stop a plant?", which sets up beats 12 and 13.
- Beat 10 (energy): the bike gag carries the beat. Only one number.
- Beat 11 (brine): ends on the bucket, a visual punchline, not a fact. The next beat (bloom) opens with a jump to a green sea.
- Beat 14 is short on purpose; the reveal should feel like a relief, not a lecture.
- Global rule: no beat is a statistics list. Each has one picture doing the work, one gag, and one question to hand off.

9. Ras Al-Khair capacity (F5, DISPUTED). Hedged only.
