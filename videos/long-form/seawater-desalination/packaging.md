# Packaging: Why can't we just drink the sea? (long-form, ~5 min)
Thumbnail and Packaging agent, 2026-11-08. Brief from Joe: story and curiosity first, strong hook, interesting thumbnail. Built on `outline.md` (15 beats, ~300 s, hook H1, ending E1) and `research.md`. No images generated, no credits spent. Prompts below are text only for a later run after Joe OKs.

Claim discipline: every title and thumbnail promise is checked against the outline beats. Where a promise depends on a claim still marked [needs check] in the outline, it is flagged.

---

## 1. Curiosity gap and intended emotion

- **Curiosity gap:** the sea is everywhere and it looks like the answer, yet a whole region of countries lives on drinking it, so why can't you just drink it, and how do they do it anyway?
- **Intended emotion:** a jolt of "wait, really?" surprise that turns into amused dread (the sea is free, but the drink is not), and the satisfaction of a clean answer by the end.

## 3. Thumbnail concepts (3, 16:9)

Shared rules for all three:
- One focal subject: the mascot, large, head and shoulders filling about half the frame height.
- Strong readable expression: one big emotion per concept (see each).
- Text: 2-3 words, drawn locally, white with a thick black outline (keeps the scarf the only yellow in the frame; the Short used yellow text, which is a choice for Joe). Text stays between 12% and 88% of the frame height and sits left or top-left so it does not sit on the mascot's face.
- YouTube's duration label sits bottom-right. Keep the face and any key object out of the bottom-right ~15% of the frame.
- Scarf is the only yellow in the art. Contrast comes from the blue or teal sea against the yellow scarf.
- No real brand logos, no real named plant, no signage, no lettering in the art.
- Test at phone size: the thumbnail must still read at about 170 x 96 px.

Female character: not natural for this story (the narration is "you", no partner, no named people), so none is used. All three concepts are tier 0 with no romantic or suggestive element.

### Concept A: "Castaway shock" (surprise and dread)
- **Tier:** 0
- **Character positioning:** right half of the frame, waist up, slightly tilted toward the viewer. Only character.
- **Expression and body language:** mouth wide in a horrified scream, eyes huge black dots, sweat drops, the cup held out toward the viewer as if it just bit him.
- **Clothing and appearance:** torn brown shorts (same castaway costume as the Short), bright yellow scarf, no other accessories.
- **Background:** a tiny sandy rock island, one palm at the far left edge, a bright blue sky with a low orange sun, deep blue sea to the horizon.
- **Main focal point:** the tin cup of seawater and the scream, in one line.
- **Text (optional):** "TOO SALTY?" top-left (2 words, adds a salt angle that the title does not say).
- **Visual question:** why is the cup of sea a problem when the sea is everywhere?
- **Why it could attract clicks:** a single clear image of a problem, a strong expression, and the cold-open question. Immediate.
- **Where the promise appears in the video:** cold open (0:00-0:20) and salt bars in beat 2 (0:20-0:40).
- **Risk:** this is the same castaway composition as the approved Short thumbnail. It reads as "the Short, longer", which helps continuity but gives less novelty.

### Concept B: "Gulf plant vs castaway" (suspicion and awe, the contrast)
- **Tier:** 0
- **Character positioning:** lower-left foreground, three-quarter view, small-to-medium in frame, leaning back.
- **Expression and body language:** narrowed eyes with one raised eyebrow, lips pressed tight, a big sweat drop, glass of clear water held out at arm's length toward the viewer with a wary stance.
- **Clothing and appearance:** same scarf, same torn shorts, no other changes.
- **Background:** a generic (invented) glossy desalination plant filling the right two-thirds at sunset: silver pipes running into the sea, intake pipes with white foam, domed tanks with warm lit windows, white steam clouds, violet and orange sky. The sand is orange-brown, not yellow. The sea is teal.
- **Main focal point:** the glass in his hand and his suspicious face, against the huge plant.
- **Text (optional):** "WORTH IT?" top-left (2 words; the title says "costs fuel", so the text does not repeat it).
- **Visual question:** the sea is free, so why does a whole plant exist to drink it, and is it worth the price?
- **Why it could attract clicks:** the scale contrast (one small man, one huge plant) and the glass of water, which says "it works". The scarf pops against the teal sea and the orange dunes.
- **Where the promise appears in the video:** beat 8 (the plant at 2:40), beat 10 (the energy catch at 3:20), and the answer in beat 14 (4:30).
- **Risk:** the sunset-plant background is busy. Keep the plant in soft detail behind the mascot so the face stays the focal point.

### Concept C: "The sea fights back" (anger and fear, fragility)
- **Tier:** 0
- **Character positioning:** right third, head and shoulders large (about half the frame height), facing left toward the threat.
- **Expression and body language:** furious shout, eyes wide, both thin stick arms flung up in fists, sweat drops and motion lines.
- **Clothing and appearance:** scarf, torn shorts, no other changes.
- **Background:** a dark teal sea turned green and rust-red by an algae bloom, long green algae strands creeping over a large grey intake grate and a metal pipe; a dial with its needle at zero; a dusk sky in deep purple and orange.
- **Main focal point:** the scream plus the algae strands creeping toward the pipe.
- **Text (optional):** "SEA FIGHTS BACK" top-left (3 words; the title does not use "fights back").
- **Visual question:** what can stop the taps, if the sea itself turns against the plant?
- **Why it could attract clicks:** strongest emotion of the three (anger), clear threat, and an intuitive "the sea stopped the taps" image.
- **Where the promise appears in the video:** beat 9 (stakes at 3:05), beat 12 (the 2008-09 bloom at 3:55), beat 13 (the 1991 slick at 4:15).
- **Risk:** it is the most negative of the three. The art must stay non-graphic: no dead fish, no gore, no visible damage to people.

## 5. Why the title and thumbnail work together

- Title first: the viewer reads the question or the promise. The thumbnail then gives the picture of the answer's stakes.
- Each thumbnail text adds a new word to its title, never repeats one. A: "TOO SALTY?" adds salt to T1. B: "WORTH IT?" adds value to T2. C: "SEA FIGHTS BACK" adds the threat to T3.
- The title says what the viewer will learn. The thumbnail says what it feels like. Concept A makes the viewer feel the shock of the question, B shows the scale of the answer, C shows the stakes.

## 9. Risks

- **Concept A repeats the Short's castaway thumbnail.** Continuity is good; novelty is lower. Choose A only if Joe wants the Short-to-long link.
- **Concept B's plant background is busy.** At phone size the plant may read as clutter. Mitigation: keep the plant softer, the mascot bigger. The first render must be checked at 170 px.
- **Concept C is negative.** It may lower CTR for a general audience. Test it only as a second-tier option.
- **Thumbnail text color.** White text with black outline keeps the scarf the only yellow. The Short used yellow text. Joe's call.
- **Thumbnail-title overlap.** The approved Short thumbnail ("DRINK THE SEA?") repeats its own title, which breaks the "add, don't repeat" rule. Do not copy that for the long-form.
- **No search data.** No keyword volume was checked for the five titles. Titles use the words viewers search (drink seawater, desalination, Gulf) but search volume is untested.
- **Script dependency.** T2, T3 and T4 each need a line in the script (see section 8). If the script drops those lines, the title is a promise the video does not keep.
