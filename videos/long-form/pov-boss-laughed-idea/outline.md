**Premise:** Your boss reads your fix out loud at Monday's meeting, the room laughs, and by Friday you're fired. You build it anyway, in a garage. Five years later he's in your waiting room, and you set the price.

**Product (chosen): the Shelf Clip.** A $1.20 plastic clip that holds a price tag on a store shelf so it can't fall off or get swapped. Boss on Monday: "It's a clip. You want to be paid for a clip." It grows from a garage (year 1: 212 sold) to grocery chains (year 4: a 9,000-clip order) to a warehouse his own company buys. Rejected: a $19 desk-cable organizer (too small to grow), a warehouse-scanner app (can't be drawn).

**Hook options (first 15 s)**
- H1 (recommended): "He's in your waiting room. Holding your clip." (8 words) → rewind line. Win signal up front, the how is the hook.
- H2: "Your boss is reading your idea out loud." (8 words) → "Everyone is laughing. Even the intern."
- H3: "Everyone is laughing. You are not. Yet." (7 words).

**Rewind line (0:15):** "To see how you ended up here, go back five years, to a Monday."

## Risks and weak beats
- Ch4 is the weakest (business exposition). Keep it to two numbers and show it as aisles and a stamp. If it drags, cut it to 35 s.
- Ch6 debt purchase [needs check]. Fallback that is easier to believe: he must pay cash in advance at your price, and you are the only supplier.
- Boss must stay specific and petty (the pitch voice, the slide credit, the drawer line), not a cartoon.
- Money figures are fiction; the year-4 loan and the mold cost need a quick sanity check.
- No swearing. Mr. Yellow Scarf wins; no gloating beyond one line.
