# What If Money Disappeared Tomorrow? (outline, draft for Joe)
Slug: what-if-money-disappeared · Pillar 3 (what-if) · 16:9 · ~5:00 · ~780 narration words · Status: outline, not approved

**Premise:** Every bank account on Earth hits zero at 7:04 a.m. You find out over coffee.
**Through-line object:** your debit card. Declined at the café, used as a shim at home, used as a deposit at the pump, then the thing that works again at the end.
**Main question:** Will money come back, and who ends up holding it? Closes at the payoff only.

## Hook options (first 15 s, premise in line 1)
1. **Recommended:** "Your card just got declined. For coffee." Barista turns your cash over, laughs: "That's paper now." Then: "Every bank account on Earth just hit zero."
2. "Every bank account on Earth just hit zero." Cut to the same counter, same coffee, same laugh.
3. "Money stopped working. You find out over coffee." Trade-off: moodier, slower to a joke.

## Motif ledger
- Card tapped at a reader: beats 0, 2, 6 (never back to back).
- Handwritten notes: beats 2, 4.
- Counting or handing over goods: beats 2, 3, 5.
- Kinds in order: scene, object, scene, object, map, scene, scene. Never three of one kind in a row.

## Ending options
1. **Recommended: the approved card.** At 10 years the card works again. Nobody explains why the zero turned back into a number, and you pay for coffee priced in eggs. Payoff: money is an agreement everyone believes.
2. **Sting:** the card is declined again. The barista: "We only take signatures now." You pay with the neighbor's egg note. Trade-off: sharper and more memorable, but it denies the viewer the callback.

## Production notes
- No readable text on props or signs (garbled-lettering rule). Use icons and red marks; captions carry the words.
- Scene change at least every 5 s.
- Mr. Yellow Scarf is "you" in the narration; no names, no "I".

## Risks and weak spots
- **Weakest beat: 3 (Week 1).** Barter is the generic part of the ladder. Needs a sharper gag than the TV-for-eggs trade.
- Beats 4 and 5 risk sliding into an explainer about money. Keep every fact inside the neighbor's or the smokers' story.
- The "why" loop is answered with a joke. Test it with Joe; if it reads as a dodge, add one line of cause (e.g., "a bad update, the story goes").
- Mackerel anchor: container (tins vs packs) and location are only reported (~2008). Hedge or cut. Radford 1945 needs a quick second-source check.
- Chapters 5-6 use a real-feeling café and a bank card. No real logos, no real bank names.

## Confidence
High on structure and hook. Medium on Year 1 (needs the best set piece). Medium on the two prison-money anchors until the light check is done.
