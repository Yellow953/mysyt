## Premise
Ex-WWI soldier-settlers on marginal wheat land, hit by the Depression and falling prices, ask the army for machine guns against a mob of emus. The government says yes on one condition: the farmers pay for the bullets, bunks and bread. The gun is a stick-figure gunner's worst job. The birds keep winning the round-by-round fight, and the real answer turns out to be paperwork: a per-bird bounty.

## POV and character
- Mr. Yellow Scarf plays a composite gunner, a soldier-settler who was in the war and got the worst posting of his life. He is not a named real person.
- Label: [dramatization] for everything he thinks, does and says. Named real people (Meredith, Pearce, Mitchell, Green) only get what the research says they did.
- Lazy, sarcastic, big honest reactions. He is the one who keeps saying "I survived the Kaiser for this?"

## Fresh angle (not Wikipedia)
The human mechanism: men who survived a world war are now paying their own army bill to fight birds, and the emus win on the press and in parliament. The turn: the thing that actually cut the population was a cheap per-head payment to ordinary people, not the gun.

## Hook options (cold open, 0-30 s)
1. **The jam (recommended).** "Four November, 1932. The gun jams. And a thousand birds are walking towards the water." Shows the moment, no greeting. Question: why is the gun jammed and who is paying? [verified: jam at the 4 Nov dam ambush]
2. **The receipt.** "The government sent him two machine guns and 10,000 bullets. The farmers paid for the bullets." Question: how is that a deal? [verified: Pearce conditions]
3. **The parliament line.** "A politician stood up and said the emus deserved medals, because they had won every round so far." Question: what round, and who lost? [verified: parliament quote, cite as "a politician" unless we confirm A.E. Green]

## Ending options
A. **The bounty ledger (recommended).** The gun leaves; the birds go back to the wheat; a line of ordinary farmers at a counter with claim slips. 1934: 57,034 claims in six months. Yellow Scarf the gunner, now a claim-filer, says the worst deal of his life finally paid him per bird. Strong payoff of the bounty mechanism. Risk: the claim figure is single-source (S1). Trade-off: a sadder, funnier-bitter close.
B. **The round count.** The campaign ends 10 Dec, Meredith is recalled, and the last shot is a bird walking off in the wheat as the scarf guy counts the rounds aloud. Trade-off: cleaner and more ironic, less payoff on what actually worked.
