Why would an army bring machine guns to a fight with birds, and who was paying for the bullets?

## Intended emotion (one sentence)
Disbelieving laughter at the absurd setup, turning into a sting of "wait, the farmers paid for that?" and then unease that the birds were never really beaten.

- Expression and body: brows pulled down, eyes narrowed, flat mouth line. One thin arm rests on the gun; the other is palm-up in a "you have got to be kidding" shrug. Sarcastic everyman, not panic.
- Clothing: yellow scarf only.
- Background: dusty olive scrub plain, a short dam wall, a wide mob of cartoon emus walking toward a teal water hole, coral and purple sky with warm orange horizon glow.
- Focal point: the mascot's face against the gun, with the emu mob behind him as the "enemy".

## Why the title and thumbnail work together
Title 1 gives the premise (a war on emus, the country named). Concept 1 shows the absurd image (a gun, a shrugging gunner, a mob of emus) and adds the one fact the title leaves out: who paid. The viewer gets "what?" from the title and "who paid?" from the thumbnail, and the answer comes in Part 1 (the farmers' conditions). Neither says "lost", "bounty" or "wiped out", so the bounty stays a surprise for the second half.
