## Premise
You (Mr. Yellow Scarf, lazy everyman, newly married) and your new wife Alys (fictional, adult, fully clothed) have one room, a family, and sometimes a cow. The question: where do two newlyweds get a moment alone? The story follows you through the house, then up the stairs to the lord's private room, and finds out that "medieval people had no privacy" is too simple. Privacy existed, was unevenly divided, and was built out of wood, wool and a sliding door. The payoff: the door.

Cast (all adults, state in brief for the policy check): you (husband, mascot, tunic over the yellow scarf); Alys (wife, 20s, long dress and headwrap, no body emphasis); a lord and his household (background, fully clothed); family and guests (background).
Setting: a rural English upland house, late medieval. Timeline risk flagged in Risks.

## Hook options (first 5 s)
1. (RECOMMENDED) Cold open, dramatization: "Your wedding night. The whole village is walking you to bed, and the cow wants to come too. So where do two newlyweds go to be alone?" Question plus persona in one line. Visual: stick-figure crowd with lanterns, a cow head poking into frame.
2. Myth flip: "Everyone says medieval people had no privacy. Then you try to get some." Strong curiosity, but it pre-announces the reveal (the myth is the thing we overturn at 4:00), so it spends the twist early.
3. Worst-deal hook: "Worst wedding present in history: one room, one hearth, and the livestock has a seat at the table." Funny and on-brand, but it tells the viewer the tone and gives no question.

## Ending options
1. (RECOMMENDED) Door payoff: the box-bed door slides shut, the house noise drops, Alys says one line, you say the last line: "It wasn't much of a door. It was the only one in the house, and you built it yourself." This reframes the opening (the crowd of eyes becomes one closed door). Then one-line CTA in character.
   Trade-off: warmest and clearest callback; softens the sarcasm for ~15 s. Keep one joke in the CTA to stay in persona.
2. Calendar kicker: Alys checks the wall calendar, it's Sunday (or a fast day), and you say "The Church would like a word." Big laugh, fits "worst deal in history," and the persona lands. Trade-off: it ends on rules rather than the reframe, so the payoff is weaker, and the joke is tied to church content that needs the most careful framing. Use as a button only if Joe wants comedy over warmth.

## Honest-uncertainty spots (narrate these with the research wording)
- Animals under one roof: "In parts of England, archaeologists have found..." Never "everyone."
- Guests and the wedding night: "Guests sometimes walked the couple to bed. How this worked varied by place and class." Do not describe witnesses or checks.
- Box beds: "Historians often trace the box bed to Brittany, though the origin is debated."
- Church rules: "A church law tradition, going back centuries, limited when couples could have sex. How strictly people followed it is debated."
- Curtained beds: "Historians describe curtained beds in wealthy English homes by the 1300s."
- The payoff line "It was the only one in the house, and you built it yourself": this is the one line that is dramatization and must read as the husband's imagined view, not a documented fact. OK because it's framed as the couple's story.

## Risks (honest)
1. TIMELINE CONFLICT (highest risk): the animals-under-one-roof evidence is Dartmoor, c. 13th c. (research #1). The box bed is late medieval (research #4). The curtained bed is 1300s (research #6). The story's couple must live in one house that has both the old layout and the new box bed. Fix options: (a) pin the story to a late-medieval rural house and make the animal end "the old kind of house" that some families still kept; (b) drop the animal end and keep the one-room + box bed. Option (b) is safer. Needs a check of the Dartmoor dating vs box-bed dating before script lock.
2. Bedding custom (beat 4): the general support is good; the details are disputed. Keep it to "the guests walked you to bed." If Joe wants the witness detail, it needs a primary source. Cut it otherwise.
3. Church content (beat 7): keep general; Burchard wording must be checked before any quote or rule. Not in scope to list the specific rules.
4. Source depth: research is from summaries, not full texts. Two independent summaries back most claims; no book was opened. A quick check of Brundage, Gilchrist and Korpiola (or one of them) before script lock is recommended but not required for the light-check rule.
5. Policy (pillar 6, tier 1 romance): newlyweds are adults (20s, to be stated in the brief). No body emphasis, no bed detail beyond the closed door. Age-restriction risk: moderate (bedding procession and a bedroom door). Flag to Joe. Do not claim "monetization safe" without the policy check in packaging.
6. Pace: the lord's solar (beat 6) can drag if the box-bed build runs long. Trim the box-bed build to 30 s if needed.
7. Thumbnail: "Medieval couples had no privacy?" / a stick figure beside a closed box-bed door, a single expression of shock. Thumbnail must show something in the video (policy).
