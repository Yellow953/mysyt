# Packaging: McDonald's is a landlord (Sonneborn)
Pillar 5, tier 0 (neutral, no romantic or suggestive element). Date 2026-11-04. Facts only from research.md. No images generated, no credits spent.
packaging-learnings.md has no entries yet, so everything below is HYPOTHESIS.

## Curiosity gap (one sentence)
Everyone thinks McDonald's sells burgers, so what question did one forgotten finance man ask that made the burgers almost beside the point?

## Intended emotion (one sentence)
"Oh... I never looked at it that way", a quiet jolt of realization (mild surprise plus the pleasure of seeing a familiar thing from a new angle), not outrage.

## Payoff the video can deliver (guardrails for packaging)
- Sonneborn (finance executive, mid-1950s onward) pushes the idea of owning the land under the restaurants; question shifts from "how many burgers?" to "who owns the ground?" (claims 11, 14, 15).
- Today: rents $10.4B vs royalties $6.0B in FY2025, about 1.7x (claim 3); company owns about 56% of land and 80% of buildings in consolidated markets (claim 5).
- The famous "real estate business" line: movie dialogue (The Founder, 2016); the real quote is "widely attributed", origin untraced (claims 22-23).
- Coda: Sonneborn left in 1967 and is largely forgotten (claim 21).
- NOT deliverable, so packaging must not promise: "most of the profit is real estate", "Kroc cheated the brothers", a secret/hidden scheme, specific markup numbers, "Sonneborn invented franchising".

## 3 thumbnail concepts (all 16:9, tier 0)
Shared rules: one focal subject, readable at phone size, the mascot's yellow scarf is the ONLY yellow, no logos or text inside the generated art (text and any logo added locally).

### The golden-arches decision (recommendation)
Recommended: NO arches in the thumbnail art. Use a generic red-and-white fast-food building with a blank sign. Reasons:
1. Channel rule "scarf is the only yellow" is the strongest brand cue on a thumbnail; yellow arches would compete with the scarf and muddy the focal point.
2. The model garbles logos; a hand-drawn imitation of the arches is a brand misrepresentation risk, and a pasted real logo next to our mascot reads as endorsement or as an official McDonald's thing.
3. The title carries the brand name; the red-white building plus a burger shape carries the category. Recognition is not lost.
Fallback (only if Joe wants it, one allowed exception): a small real-logo plate composited locally in a corner, per memory/learnings.md "Company/brand videos" (assets/logos/ has no McDonald's file; downloading a new logo needs Joe's OK). If used, keep it small, white plate, nowhere near the mascot's scarf, and the mascot must not hold or wear it. I do not recommend it for the thumbnail; use it inside the video body instead.

### Concept A: "Look down" (RECOMMENDED)
- Character positioning: large, right third, three-quarter view, crouched, head tilted down.
- Expression and body: eyebrows raised high, small round "o" mouth, wide eyes looking at the ground; one arm pointing down at the ground, the other hand on his cheek. A "wait, what?" realization.
- Clothing: only the yellow scarf (plain, no costume, to keep it uncluttered).
- Background: left two thirds, a tiny red-and-white fast-food building with a blank sign panel, sitting on a single square slab of bright green grass with visible soil edge, like a cutaway diorama. Under the slab, soil with a glowing teal coin stream running out of it. Background sky teal.
- Focal point: the slab of ground with the coins flowing from the soil, mascot's pointing arm leading the eye to it.
- Text (added locally): "LOOK DOWN" (2 words). Adds to title: it hints the answer is at our feet without saying "land", never repeats "question" or "fortune". Place top-left over sky.
- Why it could attract clicks: a clear visual question (why is the money under the restaurant?), strong readable expression, contrasts the familiar red-white shape with the odd cutaway.
- Tier: 0.
- Promise appears in video: the "who owns the ground?" turn and the coin-pipe illustration (research visual, labelled "illustration, not real data"), roughly the middle third.
- Safe zones: bottom-right 20% free for the YouTube duration stamp; keep key subjects out of it; text clear of mascot.
- Credit estimate: 2-5 credits (about 0.5 per image, 3-6 attempts typical for composition fixes).

### Concept B: "Two piggy banks"
- Character positioning: centered, medium size, between two objects.
- Expression and body: eyebrows slanted in suspicion, mouth a flat line, eyes darting to one side; both arms out, palms up, weighing the two banks like scales.
- Clothing: scarf only.
- Background: plain teal gradient, nothing else. Left: a small burger on a plate. Right: a huge piggy bank bursting with coins; a second, smaller piggy bank beside it.
- Focal point: the huge piggy bank versus the small one, which echoes rent $10.4B vs royalty $6.0B.
- Text: "WHICH ONE?" (2 words), added locally.
- Why it could attract clicks: simple size comparison reads in a glance; "which bank is bigger" is a mini-quiz.
- Tier: 0.
- Promise appears in video: the two-piggy-bank illustration and the 1.7x rent vs royalty reveal near the end of the middle section. Caveat: video must say the filing does not split profit by source.
- Credit estimate: 2-4. Risk: weaker emotion, less McDonald's specific, so it relies on the title for the brand.

### Concept C: "The man nobody remembers"
- Character positioning: mascot small on the left; on the right a large silhouette of a stick-figure man in a suit and hat, back turned, walking away carrying a briefcase, with a faint question-mark-free empty speech bubble.
- Expression and body: mascot looks after him, brows up, mouth open, one hand half raised in a "hey, wait" gesture. Emotion: curiosity and slight loss.
- Clothing: mascot scarf only; the walking figure (Sonneborn stand-in, a drawn stick figure, adult businessman) in a navy suit and fedora. No scarf on him.
- Background: red-and-white fast-food building at far right behind, blank sign, long evening light, pavement.
- Focal point: the departing man (nobody remembers him).
- Text: "WHO WAS HE?" (3 words) added locally.
- Why it could attract clicks: person mystery and a forgotten-hero emotional hook; pairs with title 2.
- Tier: 0.
- Promise appears in video: the closing Sonneborn coda (resigned 1967, largely forgotten). It is about 20% of the video, so the thumbnail promises a payoff that comes late; the cold open must tease him.
- Credit estimate: 3-5 (two characters, scarf-only rule needs extra attempts).

## Why the title and thumbnail work together
Title 1 raises a question with an unknown answer ("the question"); thumbnail A answers the SHAPE of it without words: look at the ground, the money is under the restaurant. The thumbnail text "LOOK DOWN" adds the direction, the title adds the stakes ("a fortune"). Neither says "land", "landlord", "rent" or "real estate", so the payoff stays in the video. Both work for someone who knows nothing about finance: a burger place, a puzzled character, a strange cutaway.

## Risks and clickbait flags
1. "Fortune" and "made McDonald's" overclaim if the video says the question made all the money. Keep it as "the idea that changed how the company earned" and show the 10-K numbers (rent > royalties, about 1.7x).
2. Title 5 ("You rent one") is truthful only for the structure; do not imply franchisees are cheated.
3. Concept C promises a person whose story sits late; tease Sonneborn in the first 30 s.
4. Concept B's bank sizes must stay a labelled illustration; the 10-K gives revenue, not profit by source.
5. Brand misuse: no arches, no copy of McDonald's trade dress beyond a generic red-white building; no endorsement implied; keep a description line "Not affiliated with McDonald's".
6. Topic saturation: our click case rests on the question/forgotten-man angle; CTR unknown. If CTR is low, test Title 2 / Concept C first (thumbnail-experiments.md).
7. Quote text is never printed on the thumbnail (origin untraced).
