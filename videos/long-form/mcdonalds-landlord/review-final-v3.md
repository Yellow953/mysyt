## 2. Arithmetic and the revenue-versus-profit problem (checklist 3)
Arithmetic verified, all correct. Old way: 2% x $1M = $20k; x $2M = $40k. Landlord way at $1M: rent $105k ($75k + 3% of $1M), site $60k, spread $45k, + fee $20k = $65k. At $2M: rent $135k ($75k + $60k), spread $75k, fee $40k = $115k. Keeping the old fee growing with sales is honest (Kroc's cut was a percent of sales); keep it.

The inconsistency is real. In the example "$65k" is what the company KEEPS after paying its own site cost. The real $10.4B is GROSS rent revenue before roughly $2.6B of franchised occupancy expense (lease expense and depreciation) and overhead. Can it mislead? Yes, moderately: a viewer sees "$65k net" in scene 17 and "$10.4B" in scene 24 and assumes the same kind of number, so "McDonald's makes $10B profit from rent". Scene 25 only says the filing "doesn't split profit", which does not tell them the $10.4B is before costs. Fix without complexity: use the verb "keeps" for the example, and in scene 25 state that rent is revenue before the company's own site costs. Also, scene 24's on-screen tags should say "revenue".

"Pays its own landlord": true only on leased sites (about $14.8B lease liabilities); the company owns about 56% of land in its consolidated markets. Fix with "Say the company rents the site itself" (scene 16). Acceptable because the example is already labelled DRAMATIZATION.

"Their cost for the ground doesn't [rise]": the 10-K says rent "parallels the Company's underlying lease obligations and escalations". Leases have escalators, so "doesn't" is a flat simplification stated as mechanism. The audit's "rises far more slowly" is honest but contradicts the on-screen $60k at $2M. Best line: "In our example, their cost for the ground stays put." It is true of the invented numbers and claims nothing about real leases.

## 7. Title and thumbnail (checklist 8)
Comparison:
