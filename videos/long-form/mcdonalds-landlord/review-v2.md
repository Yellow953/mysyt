## Worked example: arithmetic and clarity
Arithmetic: no error. At $1M: rent in $105k (75k base + 3% of sales), site out $60k, spread $45k, plus 2% fee $20k = $65k. At $2M: rent $135k, spread $75k, fee $40k = $115k. Old way $20k then $40k. All consistent with the fact-notes row. Letting the old percentage fee grow also follows research.md claim 9 (Kroc's cut was a percentage of sales), and the gap still widens. Keep that choice. It is the honest one, and it still makes the point.

Clarity for a viewer who knows no finance: NOT YET.
- Scene 17 packs 25 words into 12 s. The viewer hears "sixty thousand" and "sixty-five thousand" but not the rent figure ($105k, shown only on screen). They must subtract and add on their own. The gap ("rent in minus site out") is never named.
- Scene 18 hides the reason. The company's cost for the site is fixed, while the rent rises with sales. That is the whole point of the example, and nobody says it. The viewer sees 65 jump to 115 with no cause, and at $2M the rent and site figures are not even on screen.
- Fairness hole in scene 19: "You get the site, and a famous name." The name was also in the old deal (the 2% fee already bought it). The new thing the franchisee gets is the site. Also, the franchisee now pays $105k + $20k = $125k on $1M of sales, versus $20k before. Nothing says so. A sharp viewer does that sum in their head, and the "not a trap" line then reads as spin.
- "It's a trade, not a trap" is a verdict the research does not support. research.md supports the structure only. It also echoes the word "trapped", which round 1 told you to avoid.
- Optional (Joe's call): the invented markup is 75% ($105k over $60k). Sources reported 20-40% from low-quality pages. It is labelled invented, but it leans on the "squeezed franchisee" read. A gentler set: rent = $70k + 2% of sales; site $60k; fee 2%. That gives $50k at $1M and $90k at $2M, still about 2.5x then 2.25x the old way, and still growing. Arithmetic: 90-60+20 = 50; 110-60+40 = 90.

## Honesty audit
Supported and correctly hedged: 1.9% with "part to the brothers"; "by most accounts" on the thin fee and Franchise Realty; "around 1956"; "mid-1950s" and 1959; "credited with arranging the money"; "Kroc's account" for the brothers' restaurant; "one account" for 1967 in scene 34; the quote beat; the filing-doesn't-split-profit clause; "became president"; no Wharton, no 1974, no handshake royalty.

Problems:
1. Rent $10.4B and royalties $6.0B: handling. The numbers are rounded sensibly ("over ten billion", "about $10.4B", "six billion"). The sum check (10,442 + 6,018 + 88 = 16,548) matches the total research.md read directly, so the risk of being wrong is low. But two things are off. (a) The script's fact table calls these "VERIFIED via search summary". research.md 2b says STILL NOT DIRECTLY READ, keep as SUPPORTED. Downgrade the table to SUPPORTED. (b) The on-screen source tag in scene 1 reads "company annual filing", which asserts a primary-source read nobody has done. This is the video's first sentence and its hook. It is acceptable only after someone opens the Franchise Arrangements note (EDGAR, 10-K p.53-54 or the R-files). Until then the script's confidence is higher than the evidence. Fallback if the note cannot be read: use the directly-read $16.5B franchised revenue and drop the specific rent/royalty split. That loses the punch, so read the note.
2. "Last year" (scenes 1 and 26) goes stale in January 2027 when FY2026 results land. Say "In 2025" in narration, matching the on-screen "2025".
3. Scene 3 puts "who owns the ground?" in quote marks as Sonneborn's question, but scene 11 says how he pitched it is not documented. research.md supports only that he proposed owning the land under outlets (claim 11). The wording is the narrator's framing and should say so. Also, the title's "The Question" rests on this framing.
4. Scene 4 "Then he quit" implies cause or immediacy. The resignation was June 1967, more than a decade after the mid-1950s idea. The ending pays it off as a disagreement over how fast to build, not over the question. Mild bait-and-switch. Add "years later".
5. Scene 36 states "quit over how fast to build" as plain fact. Scene 34's "one account" hedge is dropped there. research.md claim 21 says to mention it is one account.
6. Scene 29 "main markets": the filing says "consolidated markets" (U.S. and International Operated Markets). The developmental-licensed markets hold about 46% of restaurants (20,805 of 45,356 per claim 2). "Main markets" is loose, though not false. Better: "in the markets it runs directly".
7. Scene 36 "and so did the rent" reads as continuity back to 1967. The 10-K supports the structure today only. Prefer "and the rent still comes in".
8. Scene 19's "famous name" and "not a trap" are interpretation (the script's own unsure list says so) but are voiced as flat claims.
9. Scene 11 visual shows Sonneborn at a chalkboard teaching, right under the narration "how he pitched it isn't well documented". Draw it as a thought-bubble idea, not a scene.
10. Scene 21 puts a Sonneborn stand-in policing the franchisee. Round 1 said not to villainize him. Make it a generic "landlord" figure, visibly not Sonneborn.
11. Narrator is accurate on "restaurants other people run". The $10.4B is by definition rent from franchised restaurants, so the script's own "slightly loose" worry (Unsure claims) can be dropped.
12. Fact-notes errors: the "What changed" list says the dirt joke was cut, but scene 14 keeps one. The header says 4:45-5:00; the table says about 4:31.
