## Title and script mismatch (the biggest structural problem)
- Title: "The Question That Made McDonald's a Fortune". Thumbnail: "LOOK DOWN". Both promise THE QUESTION and the ground.
- The script opens on the movie quote and a debunk ("Hollywood wrote it"). The question first appears at scene 15 (1:24), 84 seconds in. "Look down" pays off at 1:24-1:56. Sonneborn, the person the title implies, first appears at 1:15.
- The first 19 seconds sell a different promise (a quote-provenance puzzle), and it is solved at 0:11. The quote then returns as a "twist" at 3:23, exactly where retention is lowest (after the number stack).
- Result: a viewer who clicked for "the question" waits 84 seconds for it. A viewer who stays for the quote is paid off with "no source found".
- Fix: the question and the money must arrive in the first 30 seconds. Move the quote to the end as a short button, and make it honest.

## First 30 seconds: 5/10, a recap of a known line
- Scene 1 "You've heard the line." assumes a recognition most viewers lack. Scene 2 "Hollywood wrote it" is a shrug, not a jolt. Scene 3 "the numbers behind it? Real" is vague. Scene 4 starts the actual story at 0:19 with a salesman and a road: no number, no person in trouble, no question.
- What would make someone leave: 19 seconds of throat-clearing about a quote, then "Let's meet the guy who needed him" (nothing at stake yet).
- Replace scenes 1-4 with one concrete number and the question. Example (check against claims 3, 5, 12, 21 before use):
  - [dry] "Last year, McDonald's collected over ten billion dollars in rent. (.3) From restaurants it doesn't even run." (.6)
  - [curious] "It started with a finance man named Harry Sonneborn, (.3) who asked one question nobody in the burger business was asking." (.6)
  - [wry] "Then he quit. (.3) And almost nobody remembers him." (1.0)
- That opens three loops (what question, why did he quit, why forgotten) and delivers the thumbnail promise by 0:25.

## Worked example (scenes 16-19): unclear, and the math undercuts the story
- The research.md example: the company leases at $60K and charges $75K, a $15K spread. In the script the landlord spread is $15K. The old fee was $20K (2% of $1M). A viewer who does the arithmetic sees that the landlord model earns LESS than the old fee. The script says "plus the fee" but never adds it up, and never shows why the spread is better. The royalty ($40K) from research.md is dropped.
- Also unexplained: why a franchisee would accept this. A zero-finance viewer asks "so why pay more?" The tollbooth shows extraction but not what the franchisee gets (the location, the brand).
- The word "it" in "it pays sixty thousand. You pay seventy-five" is ambiguous on audio.
- Fix: put total income on screen, side by side. Example: "Old way: a $20,000 fee, and only if you keep selling franchises. New way: the fee, PLUS the spread, PLUS rent that grows when your sales grow. And the company controls the site." Show one bar growing as sales rise, with the old fee flat. The point is that the spread and rent keep growing with sales and the old fee does not, so the visual should be growth, not subtraction.
- Add one line for fairness: "The franchisee gets the site and the brand."

## Sonneborn as hero: not yet
He has a name, a title, one idea, and an exit, but no want, no obstacle, no scene. The research supports no scene of him persuading anyone, and none should be invented. That means the story is Kroc's thin-fee problem, then an idea, then history, then stats. That is an explainer with jokes, not a story.
What you can honestly give him: (a) the cold-open tease of his quitting; (b) one clear problem for the question to solve (thin fee, scenes 6-7); (c) the 1961 financing (credited); (d) the 1967 irony. Weave (a) in early and pay it off at 4:01. Make Sonneborn the point of view of the question ("What would you ask if you only had a thin fee?"), not a spectator.
The mascot is the best character in the video: the lazy everyman who gets the worst deal. Use him as the franchisee throughout, and make "You pay both. Congratulations." the through-line. Right now he disappears from scene 19 to scene 30.

## Honesty audit (against research.md)
Must fix:

Both contradict scenes 35-36, where the widely attributed quote contains the same sentence ("We are in the real estate business"). research.md claim 23 says the origin is untraced and the "fifteen-cent" wording suggests drift. You cannot say Hollywood invented the sentence or that he never said it. Replace with: "The movie gave the line a famous voice. Whether he said it himself, I couldn't find." And scene 44: "got a movie line with his name on it" (movie line, claim 22).
