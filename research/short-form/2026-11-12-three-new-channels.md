### The skeleton (both 28M and 33M Shorts, obs)
1. **Line 1 = a strange action with no context**: "He sawed gaps into a railroad every 39 ft." / "He put a bronze ball in a dragon's mouth... 'If this ever falls out, run.'"
2. **"You see,"** quick setup with stakes (race for a country's business / an emperor with the biggest army).
3. **Mockery**: boss turns purple, the court laughs, newspapers nickname him, he gets exiled as "the toy guy".
4. **A false failure**: the rival wins at first, or the ball drops and nothing happens. The mockery doubles.
5. **A slow escalation with concrete markers** (May: a coin fits; July: a sheet of paper; the hottest day).
6. **A disaster proves him right**, and the mechanism is revealed in one or two lines.
7. **Cut mid-sentence**, so the last line flows back into line 1 and people rewatch.
No lecture. The science is the twist, not the topic.

### Caution
Both stories are invented or heavily dramatized (fictional railroad companies; Zhang Heng was never exiled and no palace collapsed). Our hard rule says facts must be real. Joe has to decide: a clearly fictional parable built around a REAL principle, or real stories only (a real underdog inventor, e.g. Semmelweis or Wegener) told with this skeleton.
