# Fact sheet: tech-history batch (Blockbuster, Kodak, Nintendo), verified 2026-10-29
Rule: jokes may exaggerate, facts may not. "OK" = safe to state; "HEDGE" = say reportedly/according to; "DROP" = not verified.

## Blockbuster / Netflix
Sources: https://todayifoundout.com/?p=60247 , https://www.insidehook.com/daily_brief/tv-home-theater/netflix-once-tried-to-buy-blockbuster-for-50-million
- OK: In 2000 Netflix (Reed Hastings, Marc Randolph) met Blockbuster CEO John Antioco; the price was $50 million; the deal: Blockbuster buys Netflix, Netflix runs Blockbuster's online side. Blockbuster did not accept.
- HEDGE: "Antioco struggled not to laugh" is only Randolph's recollection (lip "twitch"). Do NOT say "he laughed".
- HEDGE: Netflix made similar offers more than once; the price may not be the same each time.
- OK: Blockbuster filed for Chapter 11 in 2010. Blockbuster's own mail service reportedly reached ~2M subscribers by 2006.
- DROP: Netflix market value today, exact store count at peak (not verified here).
- Nuance: many call it a reasonable call at the time (dot-com hysteria); the joke is the 10-year outcome.

## Kodak
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Sasson , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodak , https://petapixel.com/2017/09/21/kodak-said-digital-photography-1975/
- OK: 1975, engineer Steven Sasson built a handheld digital camera: ~8 lb (3.6 kg), 100x100 pixels (0.01 MP), black and white, saved to cassette tape, ~23 seconds per photo. Capture itself took far less (23 s is the tape write).
- OK: Kodak filed a patent in 1977 (US 4,131,919). Management was reluctant to pivot to digital (heavy investment, small market).
- HEDGE: Execs reportedly doubted anyone would want to look at photos on a TV (NYT via PetaPixel). The "cute, but don't tell anyone" line is a single-source paraphrase, do NOT quote it.
- OK (Wikipedia): a 1979 internal Kodak report (Larry Matteson) predicted a full shift to digital by 2010.
- OK: Kodak filed for Chapter 11 on January 19, 2012; emerged Sept 3, 2013.
- Caveat: not the "first digital image" ever, but the first handheld digital camera with non-volatile storage (say "first handheld digital camera").

## Nintendo
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nintendo , https://www.techradar.com/news/nintendo-might-be-younger-than-it-claims-to-be-or-much-older
- OK (own account): founded Sept 23, 1889 in Kyoto by Fusajiro Yamauchi, making hanafuda cards (Western cards were banned in Japan then). HEDGE: one researcher argues 1892.
- OK: 1958 Disney-character cards, 600,000+ packs in a year. 1962 listed on Osaka Stock Exchange.
- OK: 1964 card market saturated; stock fell from 900 yen to 60 yen.
- OK: 1960 Daiya Taxi in Kyoto (sold off, union trouble). After 1963: instant rice (with two other firms), Chiritory vacuum cleaner. All failed. (Years for rice/vacuum not given: do not state a year.)
- LIKELY MYTH: "Nintendo owned a love hotel chain": no evidence in 1962 filings or newspapers; traced to David Sheff's 1993 book. Use ONLY as a myth joke ("rumor says... probably not true") or drop. Not monetization-risky if cheeky and off-screen.
- OK: 1966 Yokoi's extending-arm toy became the Ultra Hand (a first toy hit). 1970 Beam Gun, 1980 Game & Watch, 1981 Donkey Kong.
- DROP: "Nintendo means leave luck to heaven" (unsupported by records).
