- 480-560 s: The turn: about 10,000 years ago farming began. Once you commit, you can't go back.

**Driving question:** "Was ancient life really brutal, and what did people actually do with their time?" The video answers with evidence, then asks the viewer to compare it to modern life.

**Escalation:** myth -> evidence -> surprising number (17 hrs/week) -> reframe of "wasted" time -> the big turn (farming) -> personal stakes for the viewer (your hours, your 90,000).

**Humor style:** dry, understated. Light sarcasm in the captions ("the movies", "the lazy-looking hours"), no slapstick in the narration. Humor comes from the visuals (red X over clapperboard, "no shopping" X over a supermarket).

**Ending:** a reflective, slightly melancholy statement about modern work. No hard call-to-action captured in the narration.

**Reusable formula (1-2 lines):** Myth ("we were told X") -> proof that it's wrong (2 specific numbers/people) -> reframe the "boring" part as the important part -> turn to a modern life cost, end on a question about the viewer's own hours.

## What we should do differently or better
1. **Build the story on one surprising number.** "17 hours a week" is the hook of the middle. Find one concrete, real stat per episode and make it the reveal.
2. **Keep the look, but make it our own.** Our target (from Joe) is the loose ink look with muted palette and the scarf as the single saturated accent. Keep backgrounds open and characters small in frame.
3. **Use red-X myth-busting and before/after split frames** as signature devices for Mr. Yellow Scarf (e.g., "the movie version" crossed out).
4. **Slow down the pace.** Shots of 4-6 s with captions held 2-3 s. The current learnings say to keep caption hold >= 0.7 s and add beat pauses. This reference is at ~200+ wpm, which is fast. For our Shorts use ~150-170 wpm and beat pauses.
5. **End on a question about the viewer's own life** (their hours, their job). That drives comments.
6. **Make the character more expressive.** Mr. Yellow Scarf (a plain round head with a scarf) should use one big reaction per scene, as this reference does with its single clear emotion per shot.
7. **Do not copy the art or the phrasing.** Use the structure only: myth -> proof -> reframe -> modern cost -> question.
