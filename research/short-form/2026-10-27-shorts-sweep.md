## (c) 10 NEW Short ideas (all >= 16/20)
Scoring: (A) random 18-35 stops scrolling, (B) understandable in <= 6 words, (C) proof it works, (D) we can draw Mr. Yellow Scarf as the poor sod; 1-5 each. Every fact below is UNVERIFIED by me; check the cited source before scripting.

1. **You Found Out Cairo's Gold Just Crashed** (Mansa Musa) | Angle 3 | Score 18 (A5 B4 C4 D5)
 Hook: "You're a Cairo shopkeeper in 1324, and one tourist just made your gold worthless for 12 years."
 Fact: Mansa Musa's hajj (1324) flooded Cairo with gold; gold price/value fell and reportedly took about a decade to recover (al-Umari's account). Verify: Britannica "Mansa Musa", Wikipedia "Mansa Musa" (note: sources differ on exact years and amount).
 Triggers: money, shock number, "worst deal" for the poor sod. Proof: money-question pattern (Originico 4.2M on 43K, Mr. Ribs 275K on 4.33K).

2. **Your Boss Is Late Paying You: Strike** (first recorded workers' strike) | Angle 3/1 | Score 18 (A4 B5 C4 D5)
 Hook: "You're a pharaoh's tomb builder, your food is 18 days late, so you walk out, and it works."
 Fact: Deir el-Medina workers struck c. 1157 BC under Ramesses III when rations were late (Turin Strike Papyrus). Verify: Wikipedia "Deir el-Medina", British Museum / Turin Egyptian Museum notes. Distinct from the existing "pyramid builders paid in beer" idea (different era, workers win).
 Triggers: power, money, underdog win. Proof: Egypt daily-life demand (History Chamber 170K on 694 subs); jobs frame (Old Oops 223K on 1.14K).

3. **The Executioner Can't Buy Bread** | Angle 1 | Score 17 (A4 B4 C5 D4)
 Hook: "You're the town executioner: best-paid man in town, and no one will sell you a loaf."
 Fact: In many early modern German-speaking towns executioners were well paid per job but treated as social outcasts (separate seating, lodging at the edge of town, shunned by guilds) and often also worked as healers. Verify: Kathy Stuart "Defiled Trades and Social Outcasts"; Wikipedia "Executioner". Keep all violence off-screen (comedy about the shunning).
 Triggers: money vs. status twist, danger. Proof: Old Oops Medieval Executioner 223K on 1.14K subs, 9 days old. Risk: topic already hot (we take topic only, own twist and script).

4. **You Can't Get Rich Selling Coffee in Istanbul?** | Angle 3 | Score 17 (A4 B4 C4 D5)
 Hook: "You open the world's first cafe in 1550s Istanbul. By 1633 the sultan wants you shut down."
 Fact: Ottoman coffeehouses appeared in Istanbul in the 1550s; Murad IV banned coffeehouses/coffee in the 1630s (popular tales of executions are anecdotal, verify before using). Verify: Britannica "coffeehouse", Wikipedia "Ottoman coffeehouse".
 Triggers: money, power, ban = ticking clock. Proof: "Would you become rich selling X in <era>" (39x-98x on three tiny channels).

5. **You Earn 225 Coins a Year (Roman Legionary)** | Angle 4 | Score 17 (A4 B4 C4 D5)
 Hook: "You're a Roman soldier on 225 denarii a year, then they deduct your food, boots and funeral club."
 Fact: Under Augustus a legionary was paid 225 denarii per year; deductions for food, equipment and burial fund. Verify: Wikipedia "Roman military pay"; Roman Army Talk / Adrian Goldsworthy "The Complete Roman Army". Mention that Domitian raised it to 300 denarii (also verify).
 Triggers: money, surprising number, reframe of boring pay. Proof: money-question pattern; gladiator "life as" Shorts (Life As A Legend 222K on 11.9K subs).

6. **Could You Survive the Taklamakan? (Silk Road)** | Angle 2/3 | Score 17 (A5 B4 C4 D4)
 Hook: "You're a Silk Road trader and the desert you're crossing is named 'you go in, you don't come out.'"
 Fact: Taklamakan is often glossed that way (folk etymology, disputed; say "often said"). Desert about 337,000 km2 with oasis route splits (northern vs southern Silk Road). Verify: Britannica "Taklimakan Desert", Wikipedia. Distinct from the backlog "Silk Road caravan" if framed as survival score not job.
 Triggers: danger, ticking clock (water), survival score. Proof: Bernard Films survive-eras 9.4M on 244K; oil-rig survive 2.8M on 81.4K.

7. **Your Dad Is in Debtors' Prison, and You Pay Rent** | Angle 4 | Score 17 (A4 B4 C4 D5)
 Hook: "You owe 40 shillings, so they lock you up and then charge you for your own cell."
 Fact: In Georgian/Victorian debtors' prisons (Marshalsea, Fleet) inmates paid fees to the keepers for rooms, food and release; Dickens' father was in the Marshalsea in 1824, and the family lived there with him. Verify: Wikipedia "Marshalsea", Wikipedia "Debtors' prison", Dickens Museum. Use a real small sum only after verifying.
 Triggers: money, absurd unfairness (worst deal). Proof: medieval punishment Shorts are the biggest demand (55M, 49M) and prisons are in our angle 4.

8. **You're the Sultan's Little Brother: Run** | Angle 3 | Score 17 (A5 B4 C3 D4)
 Hook: "Your brother just became Ottoman sultan, and the law says he's allowed to eliminate you."
 Fact: Mehmed II's law of fratricide; in 1595 Mehmed III had 19 brothers executed; later practice replaced by the confinement "Kafes". Verify: Wikipedia "Ottoman imperial family fratricide", Britannica "Mehmed III". Keep it off-screen, comic (bowstring never shown), monetization-safe.
 Triggers: power, danger, ticking clock. Proof: Ottoman Shorts demand (Knowledgia 5.6M on 2.2M subs; punishments 55M).

9. **Victorian Match Girl: The Strike That Won** | Angle 1/2 | Score 17 (A4 B4 C4 D4)
 Hook: "You make 13 shillings a week gluing matches, and your jaw is slowly glowing. So you strike."
 Fact: 1888 London matchgirls' strike at Bryant & May; white phosphorus caused "phossy jaw"; the strike won improved conditions and helped end white phosphorus use. Verify: Wikipedia "Matchgirls' strike of 1888", Science Museum / Historic UK. Keep illness cartoon-mild, no gore.
 Triggers: danger, underdog win, money. Proof: Victorian bad jobs (Glanmore 3.5M on 103K), Helen Christie worst jobs 4.8M.

10. **You Give Cleopatra Pyramid Tours** | Angle 3 | Score 16 (A4 B4 C4 D4)
 Hook: "You're a tour guide in Cleopatra's Egypt, and the pyramids are already ancient to her."
 Fact: Great Pyramid built c. 2560 BC; Cleopatra (69-30 BC) lived closer in time to the Moon landing (1969) than to the pyramid's construction (about 2,500 yrs vs. about 2,000 yrs). Verify: Britannica "Great Pyramid of Giza", "Cleopatra VII"; do the arithmetic on screen.
 Triggers: surprising number + reframe. Proof: Cleopatra evergreen (Minute History 469K on 1.07K subs; History Hideout 21M on 75K).
