## Diagnosis
1. **Distribution is the gate, not retention.** Most flops were shown to almost nobody (Starbucks 0 in 6 days, dig 2, hurricane 3). The Shorts feed seeds each new Short to a small test group; if that group swipes, it stops. With < 30 views we cannot see why, and their high "stayed" % is noise.
2. **Our hits stall at ~1K because even they are only average.** Best stayed-to-watch is 65.6% (volcano). Creator-reported benchmark for Shorts that keep getting pushed is ~70%+ stayed and 80-100%+ avg viewed (loops/rewatches); ours is 50.7% / 59.9%. (Benchmark is widely reported, not from our data.)
3. **A hit format does not carry the next Short** (≥ 2 videos, 3 formats): WW2 1,250 vs knight 44 / pirates 12; Navy 937 vs Egypt 24 / business 14; volcano 1,065 vs Sun 28 / hurricane 3 / dig 2. The premise and its first second decide each video. Other differences: topic, post date (Nov 9-10 Shorts are only 1-3 days old), title.
4. **Weak openings are visible where we have samples:** medieval privacy 20.9%, Netflix 25.0%, 1929 crash 32.1%, Kodak 39.7% stayed. These are slow/abstract first lines; the volcano (premise visible at frame 0, moving character) is the best at 65.6%.
5. **Changing format every batch** (all-day, LEVELS, money-in-the-past, why-dont-we, body what-if, cringe) gives the feed no stable audience to seed to and makes our own comparisons noisy. Hypothesis, not proven.
6. **Volume:** 2-3 Shorts on several days (Nov 2-6, 9-10). They compete for the same small seed. Hypothesis.
7. Long-form at 3-5 views is expected: the channel has ~6 subscribers and a Shorts-feed audience. Not a signal yet.
