# Script DNA: what the winning channels do that our scripts don't (2026-10-30)
Source: full transcripts read for Ink Story (WW2 day, 328K), OminiAM (Every Human Disappeared, 1.05M), Arnie Finance (Farmer, 156K), Relic Explains (Toilet, 263K); Joe's master prompt (ancient-humans doodle channel). Day One / Monk Mode have no narration (music + pictures only); SARAAB is Arabic. Ranks (Nightclub) did not load; same family as Arnie.

## What they do
1. **Scene before explanation.** Ink Story opens "It's still dark. You're asleep in a hole in the ground when somebody taps your boot with the end of a rifle. Not because the Germans are attacking. Not because you overslept, because dawn is coming." Relic: "there was a bush, and the hope that nobody was watching... Picture a street in medieval Paris, early morning... a chamber pot is emptied onto the street." Sensory, specific, a place and a date. Ours: "You wake up and the Sun is gone" (a statement, no scene).
2. **Hard specifics in every beat.** Arnie: "$41 in checking", "$1,950 an acre", "17%", "$787 by 1986", named people (Orin Heckert, 74, hearing aid he switches off during sermon; Kip the auctioneer). Omini: "10,000 planes", "400 nuclear reactors", "2 days: subway underwater". Ink Story: Normandy, July 1944, M1 Garand. Ours: one number per Short, no names, no places.
3. **Dry humor from tiny observations, not jokes.** "Across the field, a cow begins making a terrible noise. Nobody looks at the cow." / "Your last name is a credit report nobody has to run." Our jokes are punchline lines; theirs are observed details.
4. **A trailer hook (OminiAM, first 50 s).** Counter fact ("10,000 planes, a million people") -> "watch what happens when I snap my fingers" -> sensory proof ("the coffee is still hot, cars still rolling") -> 3 teasers (planes fall in hours, subway underwater in 2 days, 400 reactors in a week) -> the mystery ("the very last trace of humanity isn't even on this planet") -> "Let's start the clock."
5. **Myth line + title question + scope promise** (Ink Story 0:55-1:25): "That's the part war movies skip." -> "So what did a WW2 soldier actually do all day?" -> "one representative day, not every soldier" -> plant: "Tonight someone will say everything is quiet. It will be the most dangerous sentence you hear." (planted payoff).
6. **"Not X. Not Y. Z."** negation ladder; **"That wasn't even the worst"** (Relic) as the reframe; **3-beat promise** ("We built it. We lost it. We built it again.") and a **myth about the inventor** ("the man most people think invented it, he didn't").
7. **Every paragraph ends on a hook into the next** (Omini: "a much bigger problem has just started ticking. Day two,"). Cause -> effect dominoes, never a list of facts.
8. **Structure by clock or level**: 5:00 a.m. Stand two / Level one, the hired man / First day, week, year, 100 years. The viewer always knows where they are.
9. **Lessons live inside scenes** (Arnie: "Lesson one comes at his kitchen table. One acre grows 180 bushels..." with the math in dialogue). Not narrator explanation.
10. **Emotional line per act**: "You've just agreed to rent your own last name." (stakes + identity). Ink Story ends with a recap of ordinary jobs "You wrote home because someone thousands of miles away was living inside the question of whether you were alive."
11. **Closing echo + comment question + mid-roll CTA** (Relic: "Where are you watching from? What do you call it there?" at 4 min; ends "next time you flush, give it 1 second of thought").
12. From Joe's master prompt: rhythm "short, short, one longer, short, question"; a question every 4-6 sentences; 3 named researchers or studies; decode jargon at once; closing line echoes the first, reframed.

## Why ours feel weaker
Ours explain; theirs show. Ours are a chain of 9-10 abstract claims with one number; theirs are 1-2 concrete scenes with 8-10 numbers, a named person and a stake, escalating on a clock. Ours have no planted payoff, no mid-point re-hook, no sensory proof and a joke every line instead of an observed one.

## All nine channels are long-form (8-30 min), none Shorts. Their per-video views come from 8-30 min watch time. Worth Joe's decision: keep Shorts-only, or test one 8-10 min video in the Ink Story / Relic format.

## Rules for our next scripts (Shorts, ~55 s, ~120 words)
1. Open inside a scene: place + time + one sensory detail + the stake, in 2 sentences. No statement openers.
2. One named or specific person/object, and 6+ specific numbers/dates/places across the script (each sourced).
3. Hook = trailer: counter fact -> impossible premise -> proof detail -> 2-3 teasers with time/number -> "Let's start the clock."
4. Structure on a clock or ladder (Hour 1 / Day 2 / Week / Year, or Level 1-3), counter overlay per step.
5. Plant one line early that pays off at the end ("the most dangerous sentence...").
6. Humor = one observed absurd detail per 8-10 s ("Nobody looks at the cow"), not one-liners.
7. Each beat ends on a hook into the next; no list of facts.
8. End: recap in one sentence + echo of the first line + one question that has a stake.
