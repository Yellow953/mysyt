## The "you" (honest POV)
Documented: lay system, rank-based share, advances, store charges deducted at settlement, possible debt, re-signing, 3-4 yr voyages, the Ringgold ledger example.
Imagined (label as dramatization): the specific port, the recruiter's speech, a named ship, the exact lay you'd get, the final owed amount, the "only one" or "you owe them" ending. Safe framing: "A composite greenhand; the numbers are from real records."

## Beats (story order, with honest support)
1. 1840s-50s farm kid signs on: lay "as little as 1/350" vs captain "perhaps 1/8" (Museum).
2. Advance + outfit charged on owner's books (~$70 average outfit, Ringgold 1859).
3. 3-4 year voyage, no wages (Museum, NPS 46-month avg by 1851).
4. Store purchases deducted (Museum, NPS).
5. Return: settlement. Twist: even a profitable trip can leave you owing (Museum, NPS). Changed end state: many signed on again (Museum).
Twist strength: real and sourced, but it is a "can" not an "always". The Ringgold tally shows a man netting $54 after 3-4 years of outfit-and-advance deductions, which is arguably an even better, sourced punchline than "owing money".

## Traps
- "Most whalemen came home in debt": not supported. Say "many" / "could".
- "1/350 was your share": it's the floor.
- "Overpriced slop chest", "25% interest": not supported in opened sources.
- Moby-Dick-era dates: Moby-Dick is 1851; New Bedford peak ~1850s. Pick one year (say 1850) and keep prices and 46-month figures consistent.

## Limitations
- NBER chapter and Davis/Gallman/Gleiter and Hohman could not be read (PDF binary). Ringgold reprint read via whalesite.org (transcription of a magazine); not the original scan.
- Only the NPS and Museum pages are institutional; they are both Massachusetts-based and may share source material, so "2 independent" is somewhat soft.
- Greenhand quotes/forecastle detail not checked.
- Saturation not assessed.

## Confidence
Core hook and twist: high. Ledger numbers: medium (single 1859 example, one consul). Interest rate: unresolved.
