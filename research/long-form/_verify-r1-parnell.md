## Traps / things the "timing is suspicious" story needs
- Proven: aunt died in 1889; will left her about 140,000 pounds in trust and was contested; petition filed 24 Dec 1889, months after her death; he had expected an inheritance (per Parnell/William pages).
- Interpretation: that he waited for the aunt to die, then divorced when the money went elsewhere. There is no primary statement from O'Shea saying so in what I opened.
- Counter: if the will paid 140,000 into trust for Katharine, "the will didn't pay out" is a simplification. The dispute is how much she ultimately got (50% in 1892, or shared among siblings).
- Katharine and Harrison (1931) supply "he knew" and shaped Parnell's later image; treat as a partisan source.
- Hunter's hook "He Knew About the Affair for 10 Years" states a disputed claim as fact; change to a question or an attribution.

## Wording for uncertainty (proposed)
- "He didn't file until after the aunt died. Historians argue it was about the money. Nobody can prove it."
- "Katharine later said her husband knew all along and even encouraged it. Others say the couple hid it."
- "The inheritance was fought over for years, and she got far less than hoped."
- "The scandal didn't just hurt Parnell. It broke his alliance with Gladstone and split his own party."

## Do NOT state as fact
- That O'Shea divorced her for the money, or that he "waited for the aunt."
- That O'Shea knew all along (it is Katharine's later claim).
- That the affair alone killed Home Rule.
- That the will paid nothing, or that she got the full legacy.
- That Katharine was Gladstone's go-between (William O'Shea was named intermediary in 1882).
- Any bedroom detail, invented love letters or invented dialogue between the lovers. The Eltham "secret letters" motif must be labelled as imagined.
- A date for the bishops' condemnation.
- Also avoid "Kitty O'Shea": the press nickname; say it is a later nickname if used.
