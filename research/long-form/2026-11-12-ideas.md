# Long-form ideas, 2026-11-12 (gate 1)
Why this batch: our 3 long-forms got ~20 impressions each (untested, not rejected; ~50% retention among clickers). So every idea below copies the premise and packaging of a specific competitor breakout that a small channel got, and has to work for a stranger who sees it cold.
Evidence: competitor rows in memory/shared/competitors.md (seen 10-31 to 11-12) and memory/long-form/competitor-intelligence.md. Nothing new was watched today. Real-story facts got a light 2-source check (sources at the bottom); what-if and POV fiction was not fact-checked (v4 working rules).
Length: ~5 min each (Joe's standing rule). Note: every breakout we copy runs 7-21 min (see experiment L-len10).

## 1. POV: You Sold 10% of Apple for $800 (pillar 1 POV + 5 money) ★ recommended
- **Copies:** the POV time-jump (POV Simple Finance "Rusty Garage" 684K on 15.6K subs; Nate's Money Map 301K on 1K subs). Their premise is that you got the "worthless" thing and the years reveal what it was really worth. Ours flips it to our "worst deal in history" character. It's real, so the facts carry it.
- **Hook (0-15 s):** "April 12, 1976. Two guys in their twenties slide a paper across the table. Sign here, and they give you eight hundred dollars. You sign. It's the most expensive signature in human history."
- **Progression:** why you were in (you drew the first logo and wrote the partnership agreement) → why you sold after 12 days (each partner was personally liable for the company's debts, and you had a house) → the $1,500 to sign away any future claims → time jumps: 1980 IPO, 2011, today.
- **Twist:** in the early 1990s you also sold the original contract for $500. It sold at auction in 2011 for ~$1.59M. Two worst deals from one piece of paper.
- **Payoff:** he says he has no regrets, and at the time it was arguably the sane choice. End with "Would you have signed?" (comment bait).
- **Thumbnail:** Mr. Yellow Scarf grinning and holding a tiny "$800" cheque, with a giant glowing apple (the fruit, no logo) behind him. Red "YOU" arrow, as POV Simple Finance uses.
- **Risk:** the topic is well covered; the POV-with-time-jumps angle is our fresh part. Use no Apple logo or trademarks. Today's value of the stake varies by source ($75B-$400B), so say "hundreds of billions" and hedge.

## 2. What If Money Disappeared Tomorrow? (pillar 3 what-if + 5) ★ recommended
- **Copies:** OminiAM "What If Every Human Disappeared Right Now?" (1.1M on 6K subs): a universal what-if with you in it and chapters that keep jumping the clock. OminiAM's own flops (Oil 4.5K, Earth stops spinning 6.3K) show the premise must be universal, and money is universal.
- **Hook:** "You tap your card for coffee. Declined. You try cash: the barista laughs, it's paper now. Every bank account on Earth just hit zero."
- **Clock ladder:** Hour 1 → Day 1 (food and fuel panic) → Week 1 (barter, then what's easiest to trade) → Month 1 (something becomes money anyway; real examples: cigarettes in WW2 prisoner-of-war camps, packs of mackerel in US prisons) → Year 1 → 10 years.
- **Twist:** money comes back within weeks every time; humans can't live without it. So the real question becomes who controls the new money.
- **Payoff:** what money really is: an agreement that everyone believes.
- **Thumbnail:** Mr. Yellow Scarf at a checkout with a giant red "DECLINED" sign and a confused face.
- **Risk:** it's partly an explainer. Keep it as a story about YOU (the character) each hour, not a lecture. Light-check the two prison-money examples before the script.

## 3. POV: Your Boss Laughed at Your Idea, Then Had to Pay You for It (pillar 1, fiction) ★ recommended as the pure format test
- **Copies:** Nate's Money Map "Your New Boss Fires You After 19 Years" 301K and "Boss Cut Your Pay 15%" 232K. Within that channel, boss-wrongs-you stories hit while family or inheritance ones got 0.8-10K. POV Simple Finance's switch to "someone wronged you" took it from 2.8-17K to 60-684K.
- **Premise:** your boss laughs at your fix in front of the team and fires you. You build it in a garage. 5 years later he needs it, and you set the price.
- **Hook:** "Your boss reads your idea out loud at the Monday meeting. Everyone laughs. By Friday you're fired. Five years later, he's in your waiting room."
- **Progression:** the humiliation → year 1, broke (exact small numbers, as in Arnie's "$41 in checking") → the first customer → his company starts failing → the meeting.
- **Twist:** you don't take revenge the way he expects (pick one clean, satisfying move, e.g. you buy the company and keep everyone except him).
- **Thumbnail:** Mr. Yellow Scarf in a suit at the head of a table, the old boss small and sweating, red "YOU" arrow.
- **Why:** Joe already wants to test this format (11-12). It's fiction, so no fact risk, but the money mechanics must be realistic. Present it clearly as a POV story.
