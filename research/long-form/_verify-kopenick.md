## Verdict: STRONG (with caveats on wording)

(a) Happened as pitched: Yes, the core is certain. Date 16 Oct 1906, town hall of Köpenick, a squad of real soldiers, mayor and treasurer arrested, treasury cash taken, receipt left, caught 10 days later after a cellmate's tip, sentenced 4 years on 1 Dec 1906, released early 16 Aug 1908. Several independent sources, including a court-derived account (LTO, de.wikipedia reprinting the verdict).

(b) Beats usable in order: Uniform bought piecemeal (8 Oct) -> squad ordered on the street -> train to Köpenick -> mayor arrested for "irregularities" -> cash taken, receipt signed with prison director's name -> vanishes -> cellmate tips police -> 4 years -> pardon -> celebrity.

(c) Traps and legends to avoid or attribute:
1. "He wanted a passport but the office was elsewhere": partly legend. The court disbelieved it; historians say money.
2. "The Kaiser laughed so hard he pardoned him": the laugh is unverified; the pardon is fact and linked to public pressure.
3. Amount: 3,557.45 marks (not 4,000+). The 4,000.70 on the receipt is explained by an accounting error.
4. Number of soldiers: "about ten".
5. Funeral saluting (French) and WWI arrest: single-source color; attribute or drop.
6. Mayor corruption: it was a pretext.
7. "Cost 18 marks": unverified.

(d) Enough material for 5 minutes with the surprises intact? Yes. The strongest surprises (a nobody with a second-hand costume commands real soldiers; the receipt signed with his warden's name; the cash was in the wrong place; Germany coined a word) are sourced. Real catch: the "passport twist" must become "he said he wanted a passport, and nobody believed him", which is arguably a better beat.

(e) Shaky claims the appeal depends on: the Kaiser laughing (legend), the passport motive (disputed), and the idea that it was the plot of a pathetic man rather than a calculated cash grab (the heist was planned over weeks, with an eye on the town safe).

Competition (from hunts, not verified by me): moderate in English (one 82K video, 4 months old). German-language competitors are strong.
