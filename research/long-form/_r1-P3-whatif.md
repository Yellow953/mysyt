Design rule used for every candidate: the generic version is Joe's direction, but the video premise adds a twist (a real deadline, an exception, a hidden mechanism) so it plays as a story. Labels: [FACT] verified in the sources listed; [SUPPORTED] reported by secondary coverage, check wording before use; [SPEC] pure speculation or dramatization; [UNVERIFIED] not confirmed.

## C1. Everyone on Earth gets $1 million. Except you.
- **Direction covered:** everyone gets $1M, money becomes worthless, you're the only one with/without money, everyone a billionaire (all four folded into one story).
- **Working titles:** "Everyone Got $1,000,000. Except Me." / "Everyone Became a Millionaire Overnight. I Was the Only One Who Didn't."
- **One-sentence premise:** At midnight every American gets $1 million in their bank account. Mr. Yellow Scarf is the one person the system skipped, and for once the worst deal in history turns out to be the best one.
- **Assumption stated clearly [SPEC]:** every US resident except one gets $1M of new money overnight. Nothing else changes: same houses, same food, same number of plumbers.
- **Opening-hook image:** a street at 7 a.m. where every neighbor stares at a phone showing $1,000,000. Mr. Yellow Scarf's phone shows "$43.12".
- **Clock and escalating consequences (each follows from the one before):**
  1. **Hour 1:** everyone is rich except him. Envy, comedy. His boss texts "I quit" to the whole company.
  2. **Day 1-3:** shops can't restock fast enough and raise prices. The money is real; the stuff isn't. [SPEC, logic] Real anchor: ~342M people x $1M = ~$342 trillion of new money, against a US money supply (M2) of ~$22 trillion, so about 15 times all the money that already exists. [FACT numbers; the multiplier is our arithmetic]
  3. **Week 2:** prices jump. A sandwich costs $300. Every millionaire is now a "millionaire". His $43 buys nothing, so he's the poorest man alive. (Lowest point, and on brand.)
  4. **Month 1:** the history turn. This has really happened: in Germany in November 1923 a loaf of bread cost about 200 billion marks. [FACT] People who had savings were wiped out. The people who did fine had real things or skills.
  5. **Month 2, the turn:** the real twist the viewer doesn't see coming. Swedish lottery data show that people who actually win big keep working nearly as much: earnings fell only slightly and slowly. [FACT, Cesarini et al. 2017] But in this world many quit at once while their million melts, so the scarce thing is no longer money. It's people who show up. [SPEC, derived]
  6. **Month 3:** Mr. Yellow Scarf never had a million to quit on, so he's the only plumber still answering the phone. His hourly rate goes through the roof. The neighbors who mocked him on day 1 are lining up at his door.
- **Twist:** the only man who didn't get the money ends up the richest on the street, because money was never the wealth. Goods and work were.
- **Ending/payoff (loop):** the same 7 a.m. street, months later. Neighbors stare at their phones again, this time waiting for HIS reply. Final line, in character: "For once, I got the worst deal in history... and it was the best one." CTA: "What would you have bought on day one, before the sandwich hit $300?"
- **Real facts it rests on (>= 2 sources each):**
  - US population ~341.8M (2025 Census estimate) [FACT]: https://kmph.com/news/nation-world/us-population-growth-slows-in-2025-as-immigration-declines-census-estimates-show-us-census-bureau-population-estimates-us-population-growth-2025-immigration-impact-population-census-population-data-us-growth-rate-decline-immigration-enforcement ; https://ground.news/article/us-population-growth-slows-sharply-in-2025-as-immigration-drops-under-trump-policies (use the Census release itself before the script)
  - M2 ~ $22.0-22.3T in 2025 [FACT]: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=R0DK (FRED WM2NS) ; https://gurufocus.com/economic_indicators/41/m2-money-stock-united-states ; https://sigmanomics.com/US/money-supply
  - Weimar bread ~200 billion marks by Nov 1923, dollar ~4.2 trillion marks, Rentenmark Nov 1923 [FACT]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_hyperinflation ; https://historydaily.org/germany-hyperinflation ; https://simplestudy.com/gb/gcse/edexcel/history/revision-notes/weimar-and-nazi-germany-1918-39/the-weimar-republic-1918-29/the-challenges-of-1923
  - Swedish lottery winners: small, lasting earnings drop, driven by fewer hours [FACT]: https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/how-does-winning-lottery-affect-labor-supply-evidence-sweden ; https://eml.berkeley.edu//%7Esaez/course/cesarinietalAER17.pdf
- **Disputed/exaggerated:** the exact speed and size of the price rise is speculation (real economies would see rationing, government action, etc.). Keep "a sandwich costs $300" as dramatization and put "thought experiment" in the description (learnings A rule). The lottery study covers individual winners in a normal economy, so it can't predict a world where everyone wins; the script must frame it as "in real life, winners mostly keep working; here, everyone quits at once".
- **Saturation:** "what if everyone got $1 million / $1 billion" is a common question. At least one video exists ("What if everyone got $1 billion?" by Mr. Yenom, seen via https://recapio.com/digest/what-if-everyone-got-1-billion-finance-crypto-money-by-mr-yenom); The Infographics Show and the "What If" style channels probably have versions. I could not size them. The "except you" twist and the plumber reversal are the fresh part; I did not find that angle. Medium-high saturation on the base premise, low on our angle.
- **5-min fit:** strong. One street, one character, six time jumps of ~40 s (Omini-style clock). Easy visuals: price tags, phones, a line at his door.
- **Persona fit:** perfect ("worst deal in history" turned upside down).

## C2. In 1930 the world's most famous economist promised you a 15-hour work week by 2030. What if he'd been right?
- **Direction covered:** nobody has to work.
- **Working titles:** "In 1930, He Promised You'd Work 15 Hours a Week by 2030" / "What If You Only Had to Work 3 Hours a Day?"
- **One-sentence premise:** John Maynard Keynes predicted that by 2030 (four years from now) we'd be so rich we'd work about three hours a day. We play out the world where he was right, and find out why it didn't happen.
- **Assumption stated clearly [SPEC]:** from Monday, every job is three hours a day, at the same pay and with the same stuff. The real part is Keynes's prediction.
- **Opening-hook image:** 1930, a man in a bowler hat (Keynes, period art, no photo likeness needed) writes "2030: 15 hours a week" and seals it in an envelope. Cut to 2030: Mr. Yellow Scarf opens it at his desk at 9:00 a.m. and leaves at 12:00.
- **Clock and escalating consequences:**
  1. **The real setup:** in 1930 Keynes predicted that living standards in rich countries would be 4-8 times higher in a century and that "three-hour shifts or a fifteen-hour week" would do. [FACT quote] The money part came true. [SUPPORTED, commentators citing BLS] The hours part didn't. Why?
  2. **Week 1:** paradise. Naps, hobbies, finally learning guitar. [SPEC]
  3. **Month 1:** the TV is watched out, friends are busy with their own free time, and the days go shapeless. Keynes himself saw this coming: he asked whether we should expect a general "nervous breakdown", like the one he said was already common among the idle wives of the well-to-do. [SUPPORTED, wording to confirm in the essay text]
  4. **Month 3:** real data says it isn't just boredom. In Finland's 2017-18 basic-income trial, people paid with no strings felt less mental strain and loneliness [FACT], so free time with security helps. But the neighbor buys a bigger car...
  5. **Month 6, the turn:** the neighbor took a second job in his free afternoons. Now Mr. Yellow Scarf wants the car too. Keynes named this too: some needs, those that "satisfy the desire for superiority", "may indeed be insatiable". [SUPPORTED quote, confirm wording]
  6. **Year 1:** everyone is back to 40 hours "by choice", working to beat each other, not to survive. [SPEC, derived from the Keynes quote]
- **Twist:** we already got Keynes's world. We are rich enough. We chose stuff and status over time, so the 15-hour week was always available and we turned it down.
- **Ending/payoff (loop):** 2030, 9 p.m., Mr. Yellow Scarf at his desk, still working, next to the open 1930 envelope. He looks at it, closes the laptop and goes home at 12 the next day. Or he doesn't (an open choice for the comments). CTA: "Would you take the 3-hour day if it meant the smaller car?"
- **Real facts (>= 2 sources):**
  - Keynes 1930 essay "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren"; quotes "between four and eight times" and "Three-hour shifts or a fifteen-hour week" [FACT]: https://quoteinvestigator.com/2025/02/03/work-week/ ; https://marcellus.in/story/economic-possibilities-for-our-grandchildren-1930/ ; https://cepr.org/multimedia/whatever-happened-15-hour-work-week
  - "Nervous breakdown" / wives of the well-to-do [SUPPORTED]: https://www.rollingalpha.com/learning-to-live-with-leisure/ ; https://dissentmagazine.org/?p=14718. Get the verbatim text from Essays in Persuasion before use.
  - "Insatiable" needs of superiority [SUPPORTED]: search summaries of https://siliconcanals.com/?p=102244 and https://www.barcelona.cat/metropolis/en/contents/keyness-unfulfilled-promise. Confirm the verbatim wording.
  - Finland basic income: better wellbeing, small employment effects [FACT]: https://stm.fi/en/-/perustulokokeilun-tulokset-tyollisyysvaikutukset-vahaisia-toimeentulo-ja-psyykkinen-terveys-koettiin-paremmaksi ; https://yle.fi/a/3-11337944 ; https://toolbox.finland.fi/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2018/04/basic_income_experiment_2017-2018_results_2020.pdf
- **Disputed/exaggerated:** why hours didn't fall is debated (inequality, as Gary's Economics argues; status consumption; liking work). Present the status explanation as Keynes's own warning, not as THE answer. The essay's date is variously given as 1928 (lecture), 1930 (Nation and Athenaeum) and 1931 (Essays in Persuasion); use "1930".
- **Saturation:** the Keynes 15-hour week is a common topic for essays and economics videos (Gary's Economics has a video built on it, size unknown; many articles). I found no "what if he'd been right, played as a life" story video. Low-medium.
- **5-min fit:** good. A 2030 deadline gives a real clock ("four years from now"). Risk: the middle (months 1-3) could drift into an essay. Keep one neighbor and one car as the visual through-line.
- **Persona fit:** strong (the lazy everyman gets his dream, then ruins it out of envy).

## C3. At midnight, every human loses the ability to lie. You have a job interview at 9.
- **Direction covered:** you can never lie (made worldwide plus a personal clock).
- **Working titles:** "What If Nobody Could Lie Starting Tonight?" / "Humans Can't Lie Anymore. I Have a Job Interview at 9."
- **One-sentence premise:** At midnight, lying becomes physically impossible for everyone on Earth, and Mr. Yellow Scarf has a 24-hour day of interviews, dates and family dinners ahead.
- **Assumption stated clearly [SPEC]:** no one can say anything they believe to be false. Silence is still allowed. (The silence loophole becomes a plot point.)
- **Opening-hook image:** 8:59 a.m., interviewer: "Why do you want this job?" Mr. Yellow Scarf opens his mouth, and out comes "Rent."
- **Clock and escalating consequences:**
  1. **7 a.m., small:** "Do you like my cooking?" Breakfast goes quiet. Everyone discovers the silence loophole.
  2. **9 a.m., work:** the interview. To everyone's surprise he gets hired because the interviewer can't lie either ("Honestly, nobody else applied"). Real anchor: most people barely lie at all. In a survey of 1,000 US adults, 60% reported no lies in the last 24 hours, and about half of all lies came from just 5% of people. [FACT, Serota et al. 2010] So for most people, day 1 barely changes...
  3. **Noon, the 5%:** ...but for the prolific liars, the salesmen, the poker players, the "I'm five minutes away" texters, everything falls apart. Ads, used-car lots, dating profiles (montage). [SPEC, derived]
  4. **6 p.m., big:** politics and diplomacy. A press conference where the spokesperson only says "No comment" for 40 minutes. [SPEC]
  5. **8 p.m., the turn:** what really hurts is losing the kind lies. A Wharton study found that lies told to help someone ("prosocial lies") actually raise trust in the liar's good intentions. [FACT, Levine & Schweitzer 2015] Without them, people go silent at exactly the moments that need kindness: a friend's bad haircut, a grandma's gift.
  6. **11:58 p.m., climax:** Grandma, who knitted his yellow scarf, asks: "Do you actually like it?" He can't lie. Two minutes to midnight...
- **Twist:** he says "I love it", and it's true. The one moment that needed a lie didn't. (A brand-origin beat for the scarf, labelled as fiction.) Alternative twist for the critic to choose: at midnight the spell lifts, and the first thing everyone does is lie, kindly.
- **Ending/payoff:** midnight, ability back. The world exhales. His first sentence: "Great haircut, man." CTA: "What's the first lie YOU'D tell when it came back?"
- **Real facts (>= 2 sources):**
  - Serota, Levine & Boster 2010, mean 1.65 lies/day, 60% report none, ~half of lies from 5% [FACT]: https://oakland.edu/Assets/upload/docs/News/2014/Serota-et-al-Prevalence-of-Lying-2010.pdf ; https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/homo-consumericus/201111/how-often-do-people-lie-in-their-daily-lives ; replication https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/2021-serota.pdf
  - DePaulo 1996 diaries: students ~2 lies/day, community ~1 [FACT]: https://smg.media.mit.edu/library/DePauloEtAl.LyingEverydayLife.pdf ; https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/articles/199705/the-truth-about-lying
  - Levine & Schweitzer 2015, prosocial lies raise benevolence-based trust and lower integrity-based trust [FACT]: https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/Prosocial-Lies-OBHDP.pdf ; https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jobhdp:v:126:y:2015:i:c:p:88-106
- **Disputed/exaggerated:** the lie counts are self-reports. Say "people SAY they tell..." (a joke that writes itself). The grandma/scarf origin is fiction and must not become canon unless Joe wants it to.
- **Saturation:** owned in pop culture by The Invention of Lying (2009 film) and Liar Liar (1997). On YouTube I found a "Radical Honesty: What if we all told the truth?" video essay (Like Stories of Old, size unknown) and WatchMojo-type lists. I found no animated "24-hour clock" what-if. Medium: the premise feels familiar, so the 24-hour clock plus the "60% barely lie" turn has to carry the freshness.
- **5-min fit:** very strong. A 24-hour clock with 6 stops. Comedy at every stop, a real emotional turn at the end.
- **Persona fit:** strong (big honest reactions are literally the premise).

## C4. For 24 hours, everyone can hear your thoughts.
- **Direction covered:** mind reading, flipped (YOUR mind is the one being read, by everyone at once).
- **Working titles:** "For 24 Hours, Everyone Can Hear Your Thoughts" / "What If Your Thoughts Played Out Loud for One Day?"
- **One-sentence premise:** For one day every human's thoughts are audible to anyone nearby. Mr. Yellow Scarf, the world's most awkward thinker, has to survive a crowded commute, an office and a first date.
- **Assumption stated clearly [SPEC]:** thoughts are audible within ~3 meters, like speech. No one can turn it off.
- **Opening-hook image:** a silent elevator full of stickmen, each with a speech bubble of their thoughts overlapping ("did I lock the door", "nice scarf", "I'm hungry", "I hate this song").
- **Clock and escalating consequences:**
  1. **7 a.m.:** at home, the cat-sized embarrassments. He hears his own thoughts out loud and panics.
  2. **8 a.m., commute:** noise. Researchers estimate people have about 6,200 distinct thoughts a day [SUPPORTED, Tseng & Poppenk 2020, figure from press coverage], so a full subway carriage is a stadium of mental chatter.
  3. **10 a.m., office:** the weird thoughts come out. Coworkers hear his worst passing thought (a non-graphic, harmless, absurd intrusive thought, e.g. "what if I just shouted in this meeting?"). Panic, shame.
  4. **Noon, the turn:** then he hears THEIRS, and they are just as weird. Real anchor: in a study across 13 countries on six continents, about 94% of people reported unwanted intrusive thoughts in the past three months. [FACT, Radomsky et al. 2014] Weird thoughts are normal. Shame collapses citywide. [SPEC, derived]
  5. **3 p.m., the second turn:** nobody is listening to him anyway. Everyone is too busy with their own 6,000 thoughts. Real anchor: in the "spotlight effect" study, students in an embarrassing T-shirt thought about half the room noticed; far fewer did. [FACT core, exact % SUPPORTED only]
  6. **8 p.m., first date, climax:** a first date where both people can hear everything. Both are thinking "I hope they like me". [SPEC]
- **Twist:** the day everyone's secret thoughts went public was the day everyone realized nobody is as weird, or as watched, as they thought.
- **Ending/payoff:** midnight, the thoughts go silent. His date says out loud the one thing she thought all evening; he can finally reply with a real sentence. CTA: "Be honest. What's the first thought you'd want to hide?" (a guaranteed comment magnet)
- **Real facts (>= 2 sources):**
  - Radomsky et al. 2014, 777 students, 13 countries, ~94% intrusive thoughts [FACT]: https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/04/140408122137.htm ; https://www.livescience.com/44687-most-people-have-unwanted-thoughts.html ; https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/mental-health/most-people-experience-thoughts-associated-with-ocd-686904.html
  - Tseng & Poppenk 2020 (Nature Communications), ~6,200 thoughts/day [SUPPORTED; the paper is closed access, number from press]: https://www.queensu.ca/gazette/stories/discovery-thought-worms-opens-window-mind ; https://www.vice.com/en/article/researchers-now-know-just-how-many-thoughts-you-have-in-a-day/
  - Spotlight effect, Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky 2000 [FACT core; get exact % from the paper]: https://psychologytoday.com/us/blog/mental-mishaps/202111/why-some-people-assume-everyone-is-looking-them ; https://cornellalumnimagazine.com/things-that-make-you-go-hmm
- **Disputed/exaggerated:** "6,200 thoughts" is a measure of brain-state transitions, not literal sentences. Narrate it as "scientists estimate about 6,000 thoughts a day". Intrusive thoughts must stay harmless and non-violent (monetization and taste). Avoid the "urge to jump" example.
- **Saturation:** "what if you could read minds" is common (WatchMojo "10 ways mind reading would ruin your life", podcasts, blogs). "Everyone hears YOUR thoughts for 24 hours" with a shame-collapse turn I did not find. Sizes unknown. Medium on the base premise, low on our angle.
- **5-min fit:** strong. A 24-hour clock with six locations. Very visual (overlapping thought bubbles are made for stickman art).
- **Persona fit:** very strong (a sarcastic inner voice is literally the gag).

## C5. Tomorrow, nobody can fall in love. Everything else still works.
- **Direction covered:** nobody can fall in love.
- **Working titles:** "What If Nobody Could Fall in Love Anymore?" / "Love Got Switched Off. Marriage Didn't Even Notice."
- **One-sentence premise:** One morning, romantic love is switched off in every brain on Earth. Desire and attachment still work, and Mr. Yellow Scarf finds out how much of the world was secretly running on love.
- **Assumption stated clearly [SPEC]:** only the "falling in love" system (romantic attraction) is gone. Lust and long-term attachment stay. This split is real science (Fisher's three systems), so the consequences can be derived instead of invented.
- **Opening-hook image:** Valentine's Day morning. Mr. Yellow Scarf holds a bouquet at his crush's door and feels... nothing. He checks his pulse.
- **Clock and escalating consequences:**
  1. **Day 1:** the obsession is gone. Brain scans of people newly in love show the reward system (ventral tegmental area, caudate) lighting up at a photo of the beloved [FACT, Fisher/Aron 2005]. Now nothing lights up. No more texting at 3 a.m.
  2. **Week 1, culture breaks:** love songs, rom-coms and poetry stop making sense to anyone (a radio DJ playing a ballad to blank faces). [SPEC]
  3. **Month 1, the surprise:** marriage carries on just fine. Historian Stephanie Coontz: for thousands of years marriage was mostly about property, alliances and labor, and the "love match" ideal is only about 200 years old in the West. [FACT, Coontz] So the world simply goes back to how great-great-grandparents married: matchmakers, spreadsheets, cows. [SPEC, comedic]
  4. **Month 3:** dating apps become job boards. Couples sign contracts. Fewer heartbreaks, fewer breakups. [SPEC]
  5. **Month 6, the turn:** love wasn't just a Western invention, though. Anthropologists found clear evidence of romantic love in 147 of 166 cultures studied (about 89%). [FACT, Jankowiak & Fischer 1992] It is close to universal, so what's missing is felt everywhere. Attachment still works, so old couples are fine. But no new "spark" means nobody does anything irrational for anyone: no one moves across the world, writes a symphony or forgives the unforgivable for love. [SPEC, derived]
  6. **Year 1, climax:** Mr. Yellow Scarf, perfectly rational, in a "sensible" marriage, still saves the seat next to him on the bus for the same person every morning, and can't explain why.
- **Twist:** removing love doesn't break marriage or society (history proves it). It breaks the one thing that made people act against their own interest for someone else.
- **Ending/payoff:** love switches back on. Instead of an explosion, it's tiny: he finally realizes why he saves the seat. CTA: "Would you switch it off if it meant never getting your heart broken?"
- **Real facts (>= 2 sources):**
  - Fisher, Aron & Brown 2005 fMRI, VTA and caudate activation (dopamine link inferred, not measured) [FACT]: https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/cne.20772 ; https://einsteinmed.edu/uploadedFiles/departments/neurology/Clinical_and_Research_Programs/Reward_Emotion_and_Motivation/Aron%20et%20al%20JN%2005.pdf ; https://www.themantic-education.com/ibpsych/2017/11/01/key-studies-dopamine-and-love-fisher-2005/
  - Fisher's three systems (lust, romantic attraction, attachment) [FACT]: https://helenfisher.com/defining-the-brain-systems-of-lust-romantic-attraction-and-attachment/ ; https://helenfisher.com/romantic-love-a-mammalian-brain-system-for-mate-choice/
  - Coontz: the love-match ideal is ~200 years old [FACT]: https://www.voanews.com/amp/a-13-2005-07-05-voa43-66931797/264230.html ; https://www.npr.org/2005/06/02/4677073/marriage-in-crisis-and-the-role-of-love ; https://penguinrandomhouse.com/books/isbn/9780143036678
  - Jankowiak & Fischer 1992, romantic love in 147/166 cultures [FACT]: https://oasis.library.unlv.edu/anthro_fac_articles/8 ; https://theworld.org/stories/2014-02-12/does-west-have-monopoly-romantic-love
- **Disputed/exaggerated:** the "85% of waking hours thinking about the beloved" stat that floats around is UNVERIFIED; do not use it. The dopamine link is an inference from fMRI. Coontz's "200 years" is about the Western ideal of marrying for love, not about love existing; the script must keep those two separate (that separation IS the twist). Romance stays cheeky, never explicit (tier 0-1 under the attraction policy).
- **Saturation:** low on YouTube as far as I could tell. Only a small "What If Love Didn't Exist?" episode from a What-If series (Dailymotion, Dec 2023, https://www.dailymotion.com/video/x8qp36l) and a This American Life radio piece. Sizes unknown. The Shorts "everyone sees your crush" used a different premise.
- **5-min fit:** good. Its time jumps (day, week, month, year) suit the Omini ladder. Risk: an abstract middle (culture montage); anchor every beat on Mr. Yellow Scarf and one woman he knows.
- **Persona fit:** good (Valentine's timing is a natural publish hook, 14 Feb).

## Considered and dropped (honest notes)
- **Every human disappears / you're the last human:** owned by OminiAM (1.1M views, Oct 2026) and countless others; no fresh twist found.
- **Money becomes worthless (alone):** it has really happened (Weimar, Zimbabwe), so it belongs in pillar 4/5 as a real story, not a what-if. Folded into C1 as the midpoint.
- **Everyone a billionaire:** same mechanics as C1 with bigger numbers; "everyone got $1 billion" videos already exist. C1's "except you" angle is stronger.
- **You're the only one with money:** that's C1 seen in a mirror; C1's version gives a better loop.
