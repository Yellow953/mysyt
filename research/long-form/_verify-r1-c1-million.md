## Does each step in the hunter's chain follow from evidence?
1. Hour 1: envy, boss quits. Pure imagination (comic). Fine.
2. Day 1-3: shops raise prices because money rose 15x with stuff unchanged. Follows from basic quantity-theory logic (more money chasing same goods), widely accepted but the speed and size are uncertain. Label as "economists' logic" not data.
3. Week 2: sandwich costs $300. Pure dramatization. Real hyperinflation took years to build (Weimar roughly 1921-1923), so overnight would be unprecedented. Careful: "fast" vs "instant". Plausible compromise: scarcity, rationing, price controls, bank limits would hit first; flag that real governments would intervene.
4. Weimar as parallel: real, but cause was war debt/reparations and central bank printing, not a one-day windfall. Frame as "the closest thing we have". Real history is the strongest beat.
5. "Everyone quits at once": imagination. The Swedish study actually says most keep working; using it to claim mass quitting is a stretch. Honest frame: "in real life winners cut back only a little; here, everyone gets it at once, so even a small cutback, everywhere, bites."
6. "Mr. Yellow Scarf the only plumber, rate through the roof": imagination, a labor-scarcity story consistent with supply-and-demand. Also note the contradiction: if he has no money while prices skyrocket, he can only profit if he charges in the new inflated money; he can, so it holds.
Honest caveat: if his rate also inflates, "richest on the street" is nominal richness. Prices all rose, so is he wealthy in real terms? Plausibly yes relative to others who quit. State as a joke, not a conclusion.

## Wording to use
- "About 15 times the entire US money supply (M2 is roughly $23 trillion in 2026, per the Federal Reserve)."
- "In Berlin in late November 1923, a loaf of bread reportedly cost about 200 billion marks; the dollar was worth roughly 4.2 trillion."
- "In Sweden, researchers tracked about 250,000 lottery players. Winners worked a bit less, and their earnings dipped about one percent of the prize each year. They did not quit en masse."
- Mark the middle as "thought experiment, economists would argue about the speed."

## Story spine
Rating: spine is real. Clock structure (hour 1 / week 2 / month 1 / month 3) plus a reversal for the protagonist is a clear arc, with an ending that flips his "worst deal in history" brand. Risk: months 1-2 can read as an explainer if too many facts are stacked; keep to Weimar bread plus one Swedish stat. Saturation (per hunter, not checked by me) is medium-high on the base premise; the "except me" twist is the differentiator.
