# Verification: Tanganyika Groundnut Scheme (1946-51)

Verifier: Research agent, 2026-11-03. Discovery v2 Stage 1. No script written.
Method: opened each URL listed (WebFetch/WebSearch). Anything not listed was not read.
Could NOT access: Rizzo 2006 (SOAS eprint, host unreachable), History Today article (HTTP 403, only search-snippet content), Swarthmore paper (403), Boing Boing (403), Erdkunde Jätzold article (unreadable PDF), Hogendorn & Scott 1981 (only catalogue entry), Gunther *Inside Africa* (not found online), Wood *The Groundnut Affair* (book), Westcott 2020 book (only chapter pages/summaries read). The Wikipedia article was used as a lead and cross-checked where possible.

## Verdict: USABLE WITH CAVEATS
The overall arc (huge government plan, no pilot scheme, equipment failures, drought and clay soil, ~£36M lost, abandoned 1951) is very well sourced. But several of the funniest pitch details are weakly sourced or false as pitched: "harvest less than seed", the anchor-chain "joke", the 100,000 volunteers, the "fiasco" attribution to Gunther, and "Sherman tanks" (they were stripped, no guns). Strong primary material exists to replace them (Time 1949, Hansard 1949, 1948 government booklet, Westcott). A 5-minute story is feasible if built from the verified material.

## Beats in order
1. Postwar fat shortage; Frank Samuel (United Africa Company, a Unilever subsidiary) pitches the Minister of Food, John Strachey (1946).
2. Mission (led by A. J. Wakefield) visits East/Central Africa, 1946; report Sept 1946 recommends ~3.2M acres by 1952; Cabinet approves Jan 1947; White Paper Cmd 7030, Feb 1947.
3. Advance party flies out 30 Jan 1947, arrives Dar es Salaam 4 Feb, sees Kongwa 10 Feb 1947.
4. Old surplus tractors (many from Philippine/Hawaiian beaches) and "Shervicks" (stripped Sherman hulls with bulldozer blades) fail on the baobab bush; clearing took 8 hours per acre, not 2 (Time).
5. Drought 1949 (Kongwa); yields 245 lb/acre vs 750 estimated (Hansard).
6. Late 1949: ~50,000 acres planted; ~2,150 tons of groundnuts, against a 1948 target of 56,920 tons; auditors cannot say proper books were kept (Time).
7. 1950: Alan Wood, the Corporation's own information officer, resigns and publishes *The Groundnut Affair*.
8. Oct 1950 revised plan; Jan 1951 Cabinet effectively closes it; ~£36M written off.
9. Land later used for ranching/farming; successor body (Tanganyika Agricultural Corporation, 1954).

## Words that must change from the hunter's pitch
- "Harvest less than the seed they planted": cannot be stated as fact (#20). Replace with Time's numbers or "about 2% of the plan".
- "Sherman tank with a bulldozer blade where the gun should be": per the 1948 booklet the "armor has been removed". Describe as "stripped-down Sherman hulls" and have the character image reflect that (no gun, no turret).
- "John Wakefield": A. J. Wakefield.
- "100,000 ex-soldiers for 1,200 jobs": reportedly, or drop.
- "Managers cancelled the anchor chain as a joke": legend; drop or flag.
- "Two-thirds of tractors broken by end of summer 1947": weak; use the 1948 booklet (secondhand from beaches) instead.
- "£36.5M, over £1bn": say about £36 million.
- "John Gunther later called it...": see #25.
- "Land ended up with local farmers growing tobacco and cashews": soften.

## Honest-uncertainty wording
- "A story that's often repeated says the harvest didn't even cover the seed. The numbers I can confirm are bad enough: about 2,150 tons by late 1949."
- "Reportedly, tens of thousands of ex-servicemen applied."
- "One famous detail, that London cancelled an anchor-chain order thinking it was a joke, may be legend."
- "It's been called 'the worst fiasco in recent British colonial history'."

## What the appeal depends on, and shakiness
- Mega-plan -> no pilot -> reality hits: solid and documented (MERL, Time, Hansard).
- The "less than seed" twist: shaky. The "books couldn't be audited" and the "245 vs 750 lb" beats are stronger and primary-sourced.
- Sherman "tanks": real, but stripped.
- The humour risks: the Tanganyikans were employed, not the joke; baobab/clearing of sacred sites matters. Keep the target on the London planners. MERL notes the project's own 1950 report ignored African voices.

## Enough material for ~5 min?
Yes. Strong primary evidence (Time 1949, Hansard July 1949, 1948 booklet, Westcott on the first party and the "Half London" ending). Anchoring on those rather than the viral anecdotes keeps it factual and still funny. Surprises remain: the 1948 government booklet boasting about the very machines that failed ("Not Just Peanuts") is a good irony, as is the minister holding 245 lb/acre while defending 750.

## Research limitations
- Scholarly core (Westcott book chapters, Rizzo, Hogendorn & Scott, Wood) not read in full. Several figures rest on Time 1949, Hansard and the 1948 booklet (which is government promotion, plans not results).
- Wikipedia's article (a lead) is the only source for #8, #10, #13 partly, #14, #15.
- The seed claim and the Gunther quote could not be resolved. A follow-up pass should open Hogendorn & Scott (JSTOR 10.2307/3601296) and Westcott ch. 5-9.
