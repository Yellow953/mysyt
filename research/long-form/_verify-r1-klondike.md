## The "you" (honest POV)
Documented: Seattle/SF gold ships July 1897; rule of a year's supplies enforced by Mounties at the summit by Feb 1898; ~1,500-step ice staircase with a fee; dozens of relay trips; the cache-and-relay method; avalanche 3 April 1898; Berton's outcome estimates; prices.
Imagined: any specific person, your route choice, the scale/trip count for you, arrival date, the claim you'd find, ending.
Note on twist: it is only accurate for a person who left in autumn 1897 or 1898 (the realistic stampede). A viewer-protagonist arriving in 1898 would find Bonanza and Eldorado staked; but later creeks and "laying" (leasing) work existed and some got paid as laborers. Don't say there was "no chance".

## Beats (order)
Aug 1896 strike (claims) -> 15/17 July 1897 gold ships -> you set out (autumn 1897/early 1898) -> Mounties' rule (rule in force by Feb 1898) -> relay trips up Golden Stairs -> avalanche 3 Apr 1898 (optional, somber) -> Bennett and boat -> arrive Dawson (summer 1898) -> twist: the best ground was claimed nearly two years earlier -> Berton's outcome numbers -> Nome 1899.
Enough credible material for ~5 min: yes.

## Traps
- "1,095 lb = a ton": no, food only.
- "100,000 set out, 30,000 arrived": range is 30-40k.
- "all creeks staked": only Bonanza confirmed.
- "Ton of gold headline": body text, and understated.
- "The Mounties demanded it because of Canada's greed": wrong; stated reason is famine prevention (NPS).
- "Trip counter 30": soft.

## Limitations
- Berton's book, Friesen's Parks Canada study (403/binary), Yukon Archives (403), NPS chronology (network error), Library and Archives Canada were NOT accessed. Berton figures are via Wikipedia footnotes; I read only the first 100,000 characters of Wikipedia's Klondike article.
- Date and author of the order not found.
- Saturation not assessed; hunter noted known topic (NFB, Weird History).

## Confidence
Rule and outcome numbers: high-medium. Twist as phrased in the pitch: medium (needs re-wording). Dollar profit beats: low until sourced.
