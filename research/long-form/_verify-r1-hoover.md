## Key beats in order (usable)
1. Hoover UK (Maytag-owned) in a slump needs sales (1987-92).
2. Early 1991 a travel firm (JSI) pitches free flights; August 1992 launch with a GBP 100 purchase threshold.
3. Nov 1992 US destinations added; demand explodes. People buy cheap vacuums only for the tickets.
4. Redemption rules made hard; the travel partner collapses.
5. 1993: unhappy customers organise; one customer blockades a Hoover van with his horsebox (single source); campaigners attend Maytag's Iowa meeting (weak source).
6. 1993: Maytag takes a $50M special charge, $60.4M total free-flight charges for the year (10-K). Three Hoover Europe executives fired.
7. Lawsuits to 1998. 1995 Hoover Europe sold to Candy (about $170M, about $130M book loss).

## Disputes and traps
- Cost range: $60.4M (Maytag 10-K, 1993 charges), GBP 48-50M (weak), GBP 100M+ (Hustle estimate, no method), $72M (Hustle). Present as "Maytag's own filing shows $60 million in charges in 1993; later estimates run higher".
- "Hoover was destroyed by this" is overstated: Hoover US/Maytag survived; only Hoover Europe was sold. Also the company already had falling profits and market share.
- Some retellings say Hoover "went bankrupt": false. Not supported.
- The "ambush at the shareholder meeting" is the hunter's drama; sources only say the campaigners raised concerns with the CEO.

## Enough material for about 5 minutes?
Yes. Setup, escalation, comic customer stories, a hard number (the 10-K), a clean ending. Surprises survive: 6x value vs price, 300k vs 50k, Maytag's own filing, a sale for a fraction.
