## Traps / popular myths
- The brandy line and "very fat" quote: Malmesbury diary, unverified. 
- "Coronation snub killed her / she fell ill that night": not supported.
- "Pergami was her lover": not proven; the evidence was hearsay from compromised witnesses. Even the shared-tent detail has an innocent explanation given by Hownam (pirates).
- Wikipedia's modern-value conversions of 630,000 and 243,000 pounds are estimates.
- Investigation date: 1806 (usual) vs 1804 (George IV page): resolve in ODNB.
- "George put his wife on trial": the bill was a government measure pushed by George; Liverpool led it. Say "his government."

## Wording for uncertainty (proposed)
- "Whether she and Pergami were lovers was never proven. The witnesses against her were so shaky that one said 'I don't remember' more than 200 times."
- "Lord Malmesbury's diary reportedly has him asking for brandy."
- "He was, by two biographers' accounts, drunk at the wedding."
- "Nine votes. They won, and the government dropped the bill anyway."
- "She died 19 days after the coronation. What exactly killed her isn't settled." (confirm before use)

## Do NOT state as fact
- That Caroline and Pergami were lovers, or any bedroom/bath/kiss detail.
- That the coronation snub killed her, or that she fell ill "that night."
- The exact brandy/portrait quotes.
- That George was a bigamist.
- That George personally ran the Milan commission (not verified).
- The 125,000 pounds figure.
