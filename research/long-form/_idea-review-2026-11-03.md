## Top 3 as submitted

### 1. nauru-bird-poop-island: REVISE (demote; not a #1)
- Saturated? Yes, moderately to heavily. "The richest country that went broke" is a staple of money/geography YouTube (explainers, Vox-style, Wendover-style). The card's own "reject-if-generic" test fails unless told through one person, and the card admits that would be invented.
- Mechanism mismatch. The pitch borrows the Arnie farmer loop (wound, price tag, buy it back). Arnie's loop is a person who wins the farm back. Nauru never buys anything back; it is a decline with scattered schemes. Calling it "the same loop mechanism" is a stretch, and the Arnie evidence is itself hypothesis.
- 5-minute fit is poor. Phosphate colonial mining, independence, royalties, trust fund collapse, bad investments, passports/banking, refugee detention, health crisis: this is a 10 to 15 minute topic. Five minutes becomes the statistics pile the playbook bans.
- Tone risk: the "worst deal" joke lands on real people suffering a health and economic crisis, and on a colonial history (Nauruans got very little until the late 1960s, NV). Sarcasm needs care.
- Framing: "bird poop island" is catchy but a simplification. The deposits are phosphate rock associated with ancient seabird guano plus coral limestone processes (NV, scholarly detail disputed). "Sold the ground to get rich" also oversimplifies; the colonial powers mined it first (NV). "Tried to buy its future back" is the scout's invention.
- Needs verification: peak per-capita income claim ("among the world's richest" in the 1970s), dates, the trust fund and property buys (Melbourne tower, a West End musical, NV), share of island mined (often quoted around 80 percent, NV), passport and offshore banking episodes, Australian detention centre.
- If kept: pick one object (a trust-fund skyscraper or a flop musical) and one 5-minute question, e.g. "where did the money go". Do not do a country biography.

### 2. onoda-29-years: KEEP with REVISE angle (moves to #2)
- Strongest emotional shape of the three: one man, one place, a day counter, a satisfying end (his former commanding officer reading the order to stop, in 1974, NV). The counter fits the OminiAM mechanism (that mechanism evidence is METADATA only).
- Saturated? Yes, among the most retold. Documentary shorts, Herzog's book, a 2021 French film, hundreds of videos (NV on counts). Originality 3 is generous. The fresh angle must be explicit: e.g. the story as a sequence of "proofs the war was over that he explained away" (leaflets, newspapers, his family's voices on loudspeaker, search parties). That is the real hook: a man who can out-argue every piece of evidence.
- The card's premise is slightly wrong: "no one he trusts says it is over". Many people tried; he distrusted them as tricks (NV). The turning point was a young traveller (Norio Suzuki) finding him, then the commander (Taniguchi) being flown in (NV). Dates and names NV.
- Biggest honesty/brand issue: Onoda and his cell are widely reported to have killed Filipino civilians and police on Lubang over the years (figures commonly cited around 30, NV). A "persistence" story that omits this misleads; one that includes it is no longer a light "discipline" tale. The Day One "discipline" tie-in in the card is wrong-footed. Needs a deliberate, honest stance (not glorifying), which the card only mentions in passing.
- Persona fit is middling: a lazy sarcastic everyman is a strange vessel for a tragedy. Make the mascot the observer, not the soldier, or keep jokes about the absurd stubbornness, not the war.
- 5-minute fit: realistic and good (the best of the three).
- NV: companions' fates (one surrendered about 1950, others killed 1954 and 1972), the 1974 surrender date, 29 years versus about 10,000+ days.

### 3. lustig-sold-eiffel-tower-twice: REVISE (SWAP out of top 3; keep as reserve)
- Saturated: yes, heavily, and the scout admits it. It is a "greatest cons" staple. Originality 2 and the pick still stands, which is the problem.
- Title "sold twice" is shaky. The usual account: he pitched scrap dealers in 1925 at a Paris hotel, one mark (Poisson, NV) paid, was too embarrassed to go to police, then Lustig tried a second group who did report it and he fled to the US (NV). Whether "sold twice" is accurate depends on the second attempt being a completed sale, which most tellings do not support. Do not carry that title without checking.
- Evidence note: much of the colourful detail (fake ministry letter, bribe, wife suspecting, the mark's shame) comes from retellings of uncertain provenance (NV). Do not state it as verified.
- "The single letter that made it work" in the card is a fresh-sounding angle but is just the ministry letter; a modest hook, not a new mechanism.
- Genuinely good idea inside it: the mark's POV. The mascot as the buyer who got "the worst deal in history" is the best persona fit in the whole batch. If used, keep it as a mid-slot reserve, not a top-3.
- Verdict reason: nice persona fit, but only works if the angle is not "con man biography". As the card is written it is generic.
