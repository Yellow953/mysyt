Verifier: Research agent, 2026-11-03. Discovery v2 Stage 1. No script written.
Method: opened each URL below via WebFetch/WebSearch. Items marked "not opened" were NOT read.
Could NOT access: USNI Proceedings (McCandless 1958, HTTP 403), Joint Force Quarterly / Kostka (HTTP 403, only the abstract via search), NPS Kiska NHL PDF (unreadable binary; the NPS web page was read instead), Garfield *The Thousand-Mile War* (book, not opened), Morison vol. VII (book, not opened), NHHC Combat Narrative "The Aleutians Campaign" (not found online in this run; Pacific Wrecks and Wikipedia cite it).

## Verdict: STRONG story, numbers need hedged wording
The core absurdity is solid and well documented: 5,000+ Japanese left under fog, the Allies landed 34,000 on an empty island, men died from friendly fire, mines and booby traps. But the casualty figures conflict across sources (even within Wikipedia), so the script must use ranges and "reportedly".

## Beats in order (what the sources support)
1. June 1942: Japan lands on Kiska and Attu.
2. Spring 1943: Allied blockade; Attu retaken (May).
3. 26/27 July 1943 night: "Battle of the Pips". US battleships and cruisers fire at radar contacts that turn out to be nothing.
4. 28 July (US date; 29 July by Japanese-time sources): Japanese cruisers and destroyers take ~5,183 men off in about 50 minutes under fog.
5. 28 July - 15 Aug: Allies keep bombing and shelling an empty island.
6. 15 Aug: 34,000+ land. 16 Aug: Canadians land. Nobody is there; dogs, abandoned gear, booby traps.
7. 17-18 Aug: USS Abner Read hits a floating mine; 71 die.
8. Aftermath: friendly-fire deaths hushed (Sherrod cut), "Kiska is the invasion that fought no one."

## Words that must change from the hunter's pitch
- "Japan occupied the only US soil in WWII": change (see #2).
- "Japanese slip in while the fleet pulls back to refuel, Pips connected": keep the two events apart. Pips = 26-27 July; evacuation = 28 July; the Japanese ships were not there at the Pips.
- "The enemy had left three weeks earlier": 18 days. "About two and a half weeks."
- "Casualties roughly 300+ ... friendly fire, booby traps and mines": the breakdown is not in any reliable source I opened. Only the friendly-fire figure is approximated (25-32 dead). Don't claim a split.
- "USS Abner Read hits a mine and 71 men die" belongs to the sea, 2-3 days after landing. It is accurate but easy to mis-frame as a landing casualty.
- Sherrod "censored": say "cut by censors; Sherrod agreed at the time".
- The dogs: fine, but avoid the "dogs brew coffee" quip unless clearly labelled anecdote.

## Honest-uncertainty wording
- "Nobody knows what the ships were firing at. Maybe flocks of seabirds, maybe radar echoes."
- "Most accounts put the dead from friendly fire at around 25 to 30."
- "Counts of the total casualties range from about 300 to over 500, depending on who is counting."
- "The Japanese tried once before and turned back" (hedge: "according to Japanese-side accounts").

## What the appeal depends on, and shakiness
- "Nobody was there": rock-solid.
- "518 shells at nothing": solid (battleship number). The cause of the pips is unknown; don't resolve it.
- "Hundreds of casualties for nothing": solid in broad terms, shaky in exact numbers.
- "Allies didn't know": slightly shaky, since a few officers suspected (#16, #27). The story improves if told as "the evidence was there, and was explained away".

## Enough material for ~5 min?
Yes. Pips, escape, landing, friendly fire, Abner Read, censorship all have credible sourcing. The Japanese side (two evacuation attempts, fog forecast) adds a clever-escape counterweight.

## Research limitations
- Primary and best sources (USNI 1958, Kostka JFQ, Garfield, Morison, NHHC narrative, Senshi Sosho) not opened. Much rests on Wikipedia (as a lead), Pacific Wrecks, Task & Purpose, a biographer's blog and a Japanese-translated blog.
