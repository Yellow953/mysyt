## Does each step follow from evidence?
1. Day 1 obsession gone: follows from the (inferred) reward-system story; presented as premise. Imagination with a real hook.
2. Week 1 culture breaks (love songs): imagination, fine.
3. Marriage carries on (matchmakers, spreadsheets): rests on Coontz plus the fact arranged marriage still exists widely today (not sourced by me). The comic version is imagination. Note: Coontz says pre-modern couples still often developed affection over time ("companionate"), so "no love" cultures are not "no feelings". Do not claim pre-modern spouses were loveless.
4. Dating apps become job boards, fewer breakups: pure speculation. 
5. Month 6 "nobody does anything irrational for love": speculation. The 147/166 stat shows love is near-universal, which is what makes its loss notable; but it does not show what people would do without it.
6. Year-1 bus seat ending: dramatization; implies attachment mimics love, consistent with Fisher's separate attachment system. Label as a joke, not science.
Logical tension to fix: the hunter's twist says "removing love doesn't break society (history proves it)". History does not prove that: past societies HAD romantic love (147/166) even when marriage was arranged. The honest claim is "marriage did not depend on love", not "societies existed without love". Rewrite the twist accordingly.

## Wording to use
- "Fisher, an anthropologist, proposes three brain systems: lust, attraction, and attachment."
- "In one small scan study of 17 people in love, reward areas lit up at a photo of the beloved."
- "According to historian Stephanie Coontz, marrying for love only became the ideal in the last couple of centuries."
- "One study of 166 societies found evidence of romantic love in 147."

## Story spine
Rating: moderately good idea, weaker spine. It has a clear premise and a real double fact (marriage did not need love, but love is near-universal) which is a genuine intellectual twist, but the middle (culture montage, apps, contracts) risks becoming a list with no stakes. A spine exists only if anchored on Mr. Yellow Scarf, one woman, and the seat gag from the ending. Emotional payoff is soft ("he realizes why he saves the seat").
