# Stage 1 (v2, 2026-11-03): discover STORIES, not topics
v1 is archived beside this file (stage-1-discovery-v1-archived.md). Old idea cards in memory/long-form/ideas-backlog.md stay untouched for comparison; v2 output goes to memory/long-form/ideas-v2.md.

## Why v1 failed (Joe's verdict, 2026-11-03)
1. It generated TOPICS (Nauru, Onoda, Lustig...) from format mechanisms read off metadata. A topic is not a premise; nobody clicks "Nauru".
2. Evidence was metadata only, then ideas came from general knowledge: nothing sourced, no proof the story is real or deep enough.
3. The same agent analysed, invented AND scored its ideas: inflated scores (Nauru 27/30 was the weakest on review).
4. No click test: no title-first, no one-sentence premise, no hook, no payoff, no comparison to competitor hits.
5. 10 average ideas were presented instead of a few excellent ones; I relayed them instead of filtering hard.

## v2 pipeline (separate roles; nobody grades their own work)
A. **Watch** (lf-scout, Chrome): actually read competitor hits via captions/subtitle scan and look at thumbnails (screenshot). Per video record what was OBSERVED vs HYPOTHESIZED. If captions/thumbnail could not be read, say so; metadata is never presented as watching. Extract: why the PREMISE sells in one line, the central question, what is unbelievable, the conflict, why people stay, how title+thumbnail sell it.
B. **Hunt stories** (story hunters, WebSearch/WebFetch, several in parallel with different seams): real stories that sound fictional in ONE sentence: terrible decisions, absurd plans that became real, rulers/billionaires/dictators in bizarre situations, scams, rivalries, revenge, impossible survival, rise-and-total-loss, failed experiments. Any country/era; do not favor famous or "educational". Return RAW stories with >= 2 sources each and a one-sentence pitch; no scoring.
C. **Sell test**: for each candidate write title, one-sentence premise, 10-15 s hook, central conflict, escalation, twist, payoff, mobile thumbnail, why it could win, originality. Kill it if: you would not click the title yourself, the premise needs a paragraph, there is no escalation/twist/payoff, it is saturated with no fresh angle, it cannot be drawn in our stickman style, or it is really a Short.
D. **Verify** (lf-fact-checker): established that it happened, enough credible sources to tell it properly, which parts are disputed/exaggerated; kill stories whose appeal rests on a shaky claim.
E. **Attack** (lf-critic, independent): compare each premise and title head-to-head with the observed competitor hits; no score inflation; if nothing clears the bar, say so and keep researching.
F. **Present at most 3** (fewer if fewer qualify), with the full sell sheet, sources, limitations, and a recommendation. STOP for Joe.
Never present speculation or metadata analysis as watching; never invent performance predictions.

## v3 additions (2026-11-03, six pillars)
- Run per pillar (Joe's priority: pillar 5 gets serious attention). `lf-pillar-scout` maps channels and breakouts per pillar; `lf-scout` watches the best ones (one Chrome agent at a time; pillar 6 adds the character/attraction coding); `lf-story-hunter` hunts (one per pillar, parallel); `lf-fact-checker` applies the pillar rules; `lf-packaging` writes sell sheets and 3 thumbnail concepts per shortlisted idea (+ policy check for tier >= 1); `lf-critic` applies gates and the rubric. No agent scores its own work.
- Present by pillar, at most the few that pass the gates; a pillar with no qualifying idea is reported as none, not padded.
- Pillar 6 ideas also need the policy note (policy-notes.md) and tier assignment before presentation.
